SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: A sunny Saturday afternoon on Maple Court, a quiet suburban cul-de-sac. Three small-time burglars sit in a beat-up white panel van parked across the street from a tired beige house, going over their plan before a break-in. VINCE, the self-appointed mastermind, is twisted around in the driver's seat holding up a laminated plan, and he opens with a big, self-important "Gentlemen." MARCO, bored in the passenger seat, mocks him with a flat "Wow." Vince turns it on DUANE, the huge, soft guy who takes up the whole back seat: "Gentlemen and Duane." Duane is hurt. Marco piles on: he turns around and blows a giant cloud of vape straight into Duane's face, and Duane coughs and complains. Then Vince holds up his phone and explains the target: the single dad who lives in that house posts his nap schedule online, Saturdays from two to four. Marco reads that the dad is ex-army, and Vince waves it away. The clip opens outside on the street, then cuts inside the van for the rest. Tone: an adult comedy that looks like a family movie, with dry, fast banter, deadpan reactions and sharp cartoon timing.

Look: stylized 3D animated feature film, like a modern big-studio CG movie. Exaggerated cartoon proportions, big expressive faces, smooth sculpted forms, soft skin with subtle subsurface scattering, detailed fabric, hair and beard textures, clean appealing shapes, warm saturated colors, soft global illumination. The rendering style matches the reference images exactly and stays identical from the first frame to the last.

Format: 20 seconds, 16:9, five shots joined by four hard cuts. Natural real-time motion at 24 fps. No slow motion, no speed ramps, no freeze frames.

Lighting: a sunny Saturday at about 2 PM, the same light in every shot. One sun, high and to the upper left of the street, warm and bright, with soft blue fill from the sky. Outside: crisp, soft-edged shadows falling to the right, a bright blue sky with a few puffy white clouds, sprinkler spray sparkling in the sun. Inside the van: warm sunlight pours in through the windshield and the side window, dust floats in the light beams, and the cargo area at the back sits a little darker. The light is fixed to the world, not to the characters: when they move, the light and shadows travel across them. No flicker, no color shift, no change in time of day between shots.

Physics: real weight and gravity with cartoon expressiveness. The van is parked with the engine off and never drives; it only rocks gently on its springs when someone shifts, most of all when Duane moves. Bodies keep their volume. Faces squash and stretch for expressions, then settle back exactly on model. Vape vapor is thick, white and slow: it rolls out in a big billowing cloud, hangs in the air, then drifts and thins over a few seconds. It never vanishes instantly. Clothing, Vince's gold chain, Marco's beard and Duane's cheeks follow through and settle after every move. Sprinkler water arcs and falls with gravity.

Sound: diegetic sound only. No music, no score, no stingers. Clean dialogue with accurate lip sync, plus only the sounds the scene would really make: sprinklers ticking, birds, a distant lawnmower, the creak of Vince's leather jacket, the laminated sheet crinkling, the soft hiss of the vape, the gummy-bear bag rustling, coughing, the van's springs creaking.

12 PRINCIPLES OF ANIMATION

1. Squash and stretch: faces squash and stretch on big expressions. Marco's cheeks hollow on the drag and puff on the blow, and Duane's face scrunches on every cough. Then they settle exactly back on model. Bodies keep their volume.
2. Anticipation: every big action has a small wind-up. Vince breathes in and lifts the plan before "Gentlemen." Marco takes a long drag before he turns to blow. Vince's hand cocks back before the dismissive flick.
3. Staging: one clear action at a time, with readable silhouettes. Whoever is speaking or acting is the clearest thing in the frame.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: strong key poses (the plan held high, Marco's twist over the seat, the phone held up) with smooth motion in between.
5. Follow-through and overlapping action: Vince's gold chain swings and settles after he twists. Duane's cheeks and belly jiggle as he coughs. Marco's beard sways as he turns. The vape cloud keeps rolling after Marco stops blowing.
6. Slow in and slow out: the dolly in Shot 1 and the push-in in Shot 5 start and stop gently, and every big move eases in and out.
7. Arcs: head turns, arm gestures and the vape cloud travel on smooth arcs, not straight lines.
8. Secondary action: Duane chews gummy bears while he listens. The sprinklers and the bird move in Shot 1. Dust drifts in the sunbeams inside the van. The tip of Marco's vape glows.
9. Timing: comedic timing. Hold the hurt pause after Duane's "...Hey." Snap Marco's turn fast. Leave a beat of stillness at the end of Shot 5 before the clip ends.
10. Exaggeration: cartoon-sized reactions. Duane's eyes water enormously in the cloud, and Vince's smugness is big and theatrical, but faces never go off model.
11. Solid drawing: everyone stays fully three-dimensional with consistent anatomy and weight, even in fast moves. Nobody flattens, warps or melts.
12. Appeal: clear, readable, likable designs and expressions, even while the characters are being jerks.
