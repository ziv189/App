SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: A quiet, sunny Saturday afternoon on Maple Court, a suburban cul-de-sac. Three bumbling guys sit in a beat-up white panel van parked across the street from a tired beige house, about to go over their big plan. This clip is only the opening moment of their meeting. VINCE, in the driver's seat, tries to start things off with a big, formal "Gentlemen." MARCO, in the passenger seat, answers with a flat "Wow." Vince takes a small dig at DUANE, the huge, soft guy on the back seat: "Gentlemen and Duane." Duane quietly says "...Hey." Then Marco, without saying a word, brings his pen gadget up to his mouth, breathes in through it, lowers it, turns around and breathes a thick white cloud out of his mouth right into Duane's face. Duane coughs and complains: "What the [BLEEP], Marco?" Marco turns back around like nothing happened. The clip opens outside on the street, then cuts inside the van for the rest.

Tone and pace: a dry, deadpan, sarcastic comedy. Slow and relaxed. The comedy lives in the pauses: after every line there is a real pause before anyone reacts or answers. Nobody rushes and nobody talks over anyone. The dialogue flows like a real conversation between three guys who know each other far too well, with natural rhythm and natural breathing. The listener reacts with his eyes first, then answers. Nothing is shouted or performed.

Look: stylized 3D animated feature film, like a modern big-studio CG movie. Exaggerated cartoon character designs, big expressive faces, smooth sculpted forms, soft warm subsurface shading, detailed fabric, hair and beard textures, clean appealing shapes, warm saturated colors, soft global illumination. The rendering style matches the reference images exactly and stays identical from the first frame to the last.

Acting and movement: grounded and natural. The characters are designed like cartoons, but they move like real actors. Small, economical movements at real human speed, subtle facial expressions, relaxed bodies. A raised eyebrow, a slow glance or a tiny smirk is a big reaction in this film. No rubbery faces, no bouncing, no big arm-waving, no snappy cartoon poses.

Format: 20 seconds, 16:9, three shots joined by two hard cuts. Natural real-time motion at 24 fps. No slow motion, no speed ramps, no freeze frames.

Lighting: a sunny Saturday at about 2 PM, the same light in every shot. One sun, high and to the upper left of the street, warm and bright, with soft blue fill from the sky. Outside: crisp, soft-edged shadows falling to the right, a bright blue sky with a few puffy white clouds, sprinkler spray sparkling in the sun. Inside the van: warm sunlight pours in through the windshield and the side window, dust floats slowly in the light beams, and the cargo area at the back sits a bit darker. The light is fixed to the world, not to the characters: when they move, the light and shadows travel across them. No flicker, no color shift, no change in time of day.

Physics: real weight, gravity and timing. The van is parked with the engine off and never moves. It only dips slightly on its springs when Duane shifts his weight. Faces and bodies keep their shape: no squash and stretch. When someone breathes in, his chest rises a bit. Marco's cloud is thick white mist that comes out of his mouth only when he breathes out: it flows forward in a soft billow, wraps around Duane's head, hangs in the air, then drifts and thins over a few seconds. It never vanishes instantly and it never comes out of the pen. Clothing, Vince's gold chain and Marco's beard settle naturally after each move.

Sound: diegetic sound only. No music, no score, no stingers. Clean dialogue with accurate lip sync, plus only the sounds the scene would really make: sprinklers ticking, birds, a distant lawnmower, the creak of Vince's leather jacket, the laminated sheet crinkling, a soft throat-clear, Duane chewing, the gummy-bear bag rustling, a slow breath in, a long soft breath out, coughing, the van's springs creaking. One word is covered by a TV censor beep. Wherever a line says [BLEEP], a short, clean censor beep plays instead of that word, and the speaker's mouth keeps moving under it as if he's saying it. The beep is the only sound that doesn't come from the scene itself.

12 PRINCIPLES OF ANIMATION (GROUNDED VERSION FOR THIS FILM)

1. Squash and stretch: almost none. Only natural movement of real faces and bodies, like cheeks shifting as someone talks or a chest rising with a breath. Faces never stretch, bulge or go rubbery.
2. Anticipation: small and natural, never a cartoon wind-up. A breath before speaking, a glance before turning, a throat-clear before a speech.
3. Staging: one clear action at a time. Whoever is speaking or acting is the clearest thing in the frame, and everyone else stays still or reacts quietly.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: continuous, natural motion with no snapping into poses. During pauses, people simply hold still, the way real people do.
5. Follow-through and overlapping action: subtle. Vince's gold chain settles after he sits up, Marco's beard sways slightly as he turns, and clothes settle softly. Nothing flops or wobbles.
6. Slow in and slow out: every move starts and stops gently at human speed, including the dolly in Shot 1. Nothing pops into place.
7. Arcs: head turns and arm movements follow natural, gentle arcs at real speed.
8. Secondary action: quiet background life that never pulls focus. Duane chews slowly, dust drifts in the sunbeams, and the pen rests loosely in Marco's fingers.
9. Timing: realistic and unhurried. Leave the pauses between lines. Let silences sit. Reactions start a beat after a line ends, not on top of it.
10. Exaggeration: minimal. The comedy comes from deadpan timing and small reactions (an eyebrow, a glance, a stiff smile, a sigh), not from big expressions.
11. Solid drawing: consistent anatomy, weight and volume in every frame. Hands have five fingers. Nobody flattens, warps or melts.
12. Appeal: appealing designs and believable people. The humor is dry and lived-in, and the characters feel like real guys who've known each other too long.
