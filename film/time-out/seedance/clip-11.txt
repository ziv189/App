SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: Meanwhile, in the small, dark, wood-paneled den of the beige house. VINCE steps over the white plastic gate in the den doorway and tiptoes in, in his socks. Against the right wall sits a small black safe with a sheet of paper taped to its door (in the finished film it reads "DADDY'S MONEY" in crayon). Vince crouches in front of it, rubs his hands together, and figures an army guy must keep gold bars in there. He yanks the handle. BOOM: a huge burst of white salt bursts out of the safe and throws him backward across the room. He crashes through the plastic gate and lands in a heap in the doorway, his hair blown straight back and a thin wisp of steam rising from the seat of his trousers. He demands to know who keeps rock salt. From somewhere in the dark, BRAYDEN's calm voice answers: his dad says the real ones make a mess. Vince slowly looks over his shoulder into the darkness.

2. Anticipation: he crouches and rubs his hands before he reaches, grips the handle and holds a beat before he yanks, and goes still before he turns his head.
3. Staging: one clear action per shot: the tiptoe, the greed, the burst, the outrage, the voice.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: continuous, natural motion, landing on clear held poses: crouched and grinning, sprawled and outraged, looking over his shoulder.
5. Follow-through: the gate folds and settles under him, the salt patters down and settles, the powder hangs and slowly drifts.
6. Slow in and slow out: his tiptoe, his reach and his head turn ease in and out; the burst is instant.
7. Arcs: he flies back in one clean, low arc; the salt rains down on short arcs.
8. Secondary action: the steam wisp, the drifting powder and the room tone of the den, never pulling focus.
9. Timing: slow and smug, then a sudden burst, then a slow, quiet ending.
10. Exaggeration: kept to the slapstick itself: a big burst, a big fall, and a face that stays on model.
11. Solid drawing: consistent anatomy and volume in every frame, even mid-air; five fingers.
12. Appeal: a greedy, smug guy getting exactly what he deserved. It's a comedy.
