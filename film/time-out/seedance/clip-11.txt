SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: Meanwhile, in the small, dim, wood-paneled den of the beige house. VINCE steps over the white plastic gate in the den doorway and tiptoes in, in his socks. Against the right wall sits a small black safe with a blank sheet of paper taped to its door. Vince drops onto one knee in front of it, wiggles his fingers greedily, and figures an army guy must keep gold bars in there. He pulls the handle, the door swings open, and POOF: a huge puff of salt hits him right in the face, like a pie in the face, and fills the screen. Hard cut: Vince is now sitting across the room in the doorway, on top of the flattened plastic gate, covered in salt from head to toe, his hair sticking straight back, while it drifts down over the whole room. He yells at the safe, outraged. Then he goes very still, as if he has heard something in the hallway behind him, and slowly looks over his shoulder.

Continuity: Vince is in his black socks with gold toes, with no shoes, as in the previous clips. His gold aviator sunglasses stay hooked on his collar, as in his reference image. The safe is the same one seen earlier in the film, and its inside is never shown. Vince is the only person in this clip, and the only voice.

Tone and pace: slapstick played completely straight, in a dry comedy. Vince's greedy setup is slow and smug. The puff is sudden, and the cut to him sitting across the room is the joke. The ending is slow: his outrage, then a long, silent, uneasy beat.
