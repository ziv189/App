SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: Three quick close-ups of objects from the gear-up montage, with no people in them at all. These shots get cut between Brayden's montage shots in editing. First, looking straight down into his dad's open army footlocker in the basement: everything inside is packed in neat military rows and bathed in a warm golden glow, like treasure. Second, the dim downstairs hallway: a shower of small four-pointed steel caltrops pours down from above the frame and scatters across the bare wooden floor toward the camera. Third, the small black safe on the floor of the wood-paneled den: inside it, an old double-barreled shotgun is clamped in place with its barrels pointing straight at the safe door, with a box beside the safe; then the heavy safe door slowly swings shut by itself and clicks. Each shot is still and simple, like the gear close-ups in an 80s action-movie montage.

Tone and pace: an 80s action-movie montage played completely straight, in a comedy. Each shot is clean, still and deliberate, with one single thing happening in it. Everything is a cartoon prop in a cartoon world: nothing is gory, scary or realistic.

Look: stylized 3D animated feature film, like a modern big-studio CG movie. Smooth, slightly rounded, chunky stylized props with clean shapes, detailed but simple metal, wood, canvas and paint textures, warm saturated colors and soft global illumination. The rendering matches the reference images exactly and stays identical from the first frame to the last. Palette: olive drab, brass, steel grey, honey-colored wood and the warm golden glow.

Camera language: real lenses between 35mm and 50mm, straight vertical lines, slow and smooth moves. No fisheye, no Dutch angles, no whip pans, no zooms, no shaky handheld.

Format: 15 seconds, 16:9, three shots joined by two hard cuts. Real-time motion at 24 fps. No slow motion, no speed ramps, no freeze frames.

Lighting: the same sunny Saturday afternoon as the rest of the film. In the basement, the warm yellow bare bulb lights the footlocker from above, and a soft warm golden glow rises from inside it. In the hallway, the ceiling light is off, and soft daylight leaks in from the kitchen doorway at the far end, so the floor is dim with warm highlights that the metal spikes catch. In the den, warm amber afternoon light filters through the plaid curtains. The light is fixed to the world in each shot, with no flicker and no color shift.

Physics: real weight and gravity. The objects in the footlocker are completely still. The caltrops are small, heavy steel pieces: they fall straight down, hit the wooden floor with sharp metallic clinks, bounce once or twice, skitter a short way, and settle with one point sticking straight up. They never float, never roll forever and never pile up into a mountain. The safe door is heavy steel: it swings slowly and evenly on its hinges, slows down near the end, and closes with a solid click. Everything else stays still.

Sound: diegetic sound only. No music: the montage music is added later in editing. Only these sounds, in order: the quiet hum of the basement and the faint buzz of the bulb; a rushing metallic clatter and many small clinks as the caltrops hit the floor, then a few last skitters; the slow creak of the safe door's hinges and one heavy metal click as it shuts. No voices.

12 PRINCIPLES OF ANIMATION (GROUNDED VERSION FOR THIS FILM)

1. Squash and stretch: none. Every object is rigid.
2. Anticipation: the safe door hesitates for a moment before it starts to swing.
3. Staging: one clear thing happens per shot: the glow, the caltrops, the door.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: continuous, natural motion that ends on a clean, still final frame.
5. Follow-through: the caltrops bounce, skitter and rock into place; the door slows down before it clicks.
6. Slow in and slow out: the push-ins and the door swing ease in and out.
7. Arcs: the caltrops fall and bounce on short, natural arcs.
8. Secondary action: dust motes drift in the golden glow and in the hallway light.
9. Timing: still, then one action, then still again, like gear close-ups in a montage.
10. Exaggeration: minimal. The comedy is how seriously everything is presented.
11. Solid drawing: every object keeps its volume and shape from every angle.
12. Appeal: clean, chunky, readable cartoon props, glowing like treasure.
