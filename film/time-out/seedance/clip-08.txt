SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: A sunny Saturday afternoon, a few minutes into nap time. VINCE, MARCO and DUANE crouch-walk across the small backyard of the beige house in single file. Duane, at the back, is far too big to hide behind anything. At the back door, Marco opens the lock in about two seconds. Vince, very proud of himself, tells everyone to take their shoes off, because professionals don't leave footprints. Marco and Duane exchange a look and kick off their sneakers. Inside, the kitchen is dim, with every blind shut. The three of them tiptoe in, in their socks. On the counter, a small white audio monitor crackles, its green lights flicker, and a sing-song voice comes through its speaker: Brayden, saying hi. All three freeze like statues, and Duane grabs Marco's arm.

Continuity: before this clip, the three were sitting in their van out front. Brayden is never seen in this clip; his voice only comes out of the monitor's speaker. The three wear their shoes, as in their reference images, until Shot 3, and they're in socks from then on: Vince in black socks with gold toes, Marco in black socks, Duane in white tube socks pulled up high.

Tone and pace: a dry, deadpan comedy. The three try for slick heist-movie energy and can't quite pull it off. Slow and relaxed, with a real pause after every line. The "Hiiiiiii" and the freeze are the eerie-funny button at the end: quiet, still and long.

Look: stylized 3D animated feature film, like a modern big-studio CG movie. Exaggerated cartoon character designs, big expressive faces, smooth sculpted forms, soft warm subsurface shading, detailed fabric, hair and beard textures, warm saturated colors, soft global illumination. The rendering matches the reference images exactly and stays identical from the first frame to the last. Palette: a bright green backyard under a blue sky, then a dim kitchen in cool shadow, with pale yellow walls, a checkered floor and three small green lights as the accent.

Camera language: real lenses between 28mm and 85mm, straight vertical lines, slow and smooth moves. No fisheye, no Dutch angles, no whip pans, no zooms, no shaky handheld. Shot 4 is a low, floor-level shot of their feet.

Acting and movement: grounded and natural. The characters are designed like cartoons but move like real actors, at real human speed, with subtle facial expressions. Their sneaking is careful and real, not cartoon tiptoe hopping. Duane's size makes everything a bit awkward. Marco is quick and efficient. Vince is proud of every move. No bouncing, no rubbery faces, no big gestures. Every movement is described exactly, including which hand or foot does it: do exactly that and nothing extra.

Format: 20 seconds, 16:9, six shots joined by five hard cuts. Real-time motion at 24 fps. No slow motion, no speed ramps, no freeze frames.

Lighting: the backyard is in bright Saturday sun at about 2 PM, coming from the upper left, with crisp fence shadows striping the grass and a blue sky with puffy clouds. The kitchen is dim and quiet: every blind is shut, so only thin slivers of sunlight leak through the slats and around the small curtain on the back door, falling across the floor. The monitor's three green lights are the brightest small points in the kitchen. The light is fixed to the world, not to the characters: when they move, the stripes of light and shadow slide across them. No flicker, no color shift.

Physics: real weight, gravity and timing. Duane's steps are heavier than the others'. The lock turns with a small click, and the door swings inward a few inches with a soft creak. Kicked-off shoes tumble once onto the doormat and stay there. In socks on the smooth floor they step softly and carefully and never slip. When Duane grabs Marco's arm, his big hand closes around it and the sleeve bunches. Faces and bodies keep their shape: no squash and stretch.

Sound: diegetic sound only. No music, no score. Clean dialogue with accurate lip sync, plus only these sounds, in order: birds, a distant lawnmower, the soft swish of feet on grass; the tiny metallic scratching of Marco's tools in the lock, a click, a door creak; Vince's voice; the soft thumps of shoes landing on the doormat; inside, the quiet hum of the fridge and soft sock steps on the floor; the monitor's crackle of static; Brayden's voice through a small, tinny speaker; then near silence, with only the fridge humming.

12 PRINCIPLES OF ANIMATION (GROUNDED VERSION FOR THIS FILM)

1. Squash and stretch: none. Faces and bodies keep their shape.
2. Anticipation: small and natural. Vince raises his hand before they stop and his finger before his line; there's a breath of static before the voice.
3. Staging: one clear action per shot: the sneak, the lock, the shoes, the socks, the voice, the freeze.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: continuous, natural motion; in the freeze, they simply stop, like real people holding their breath.
5. Follow-through: subtle. The shoes tumble once and settle, the door drifts to a stop, and Marco's sleeve bunches and stays bunched.
6. Slow in and slow out: every move eases at human speed, including the pan, the push-ins and the dolly.
7. Arcs: the crouch-walk, the kicked shoes and Duane's reach all follow natural arcs.
8. Secondary action: birds and fence shadows in the yard, dust in the slivers of light and the hum of the fridge in the kitchen.
9. Timing: careful and unhurried; the freeze is the longest, stillest moment.
10. Exaggeration: minimal. The comedy is three grown men tiptoeing in socks and freezing at one sing-song word.
11. Solid drawing: consistent anatomy and volume in every frame; five fingers.
12. Appeal: three lovable idiots trying to look like professionals. It's a comedy.
