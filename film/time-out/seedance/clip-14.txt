SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: Pitch dark in the upstairs hallway of the beige house. A squeak has just come from the floor beside DUANE's foot. VINCE's eyes snap toward it, and BANG: one bright flash lights the hallway for a split second. Duane's eyes go huge and drop to the floor with a heavy thud, and from down there he screams at Vince, outraged and bawling. Downstairs, GLENN sleeps right through it on the couch, snoring, not moving a muscle. Upstairs, the light snaps back on: Duane lies on his back on the carpet, clutching his shin and bawling, while Vince and MARCO stand over him, staring. They look at each other, try to hold it in, and lose it. Marco says what happened out loud, and Vince repeats it, half proud and half amazed.

Continuity: a split second after the squeak. All three are in their socks: Vince in black socks with gold toes, Marco in black socks, Duane in white tube socks with a few caltrops stuck in them. Vince's hair is still blown straight back with salt crystals in it, and his sunglasses are hooked on his collar. He still holds the small black pistol in his right hand, unseen in the dark. Marco's pen gadget stays in his vest pocket. Downstairs, Glenn lies where he fell asleep: on his right side across the couch cushions, feet on the floor, ear protectors and mask on.

Tone and pace: a dark, dry slapstick comedy. The flash is over in an instant. Duane's scream is big, frantic and sincere: he's more scared and outraged than hurt. The cut to Glenn is perfectly still: he hears nothing. Upstairs, the laugh builds slowly. Keep every written pause.

Look: stylized 3D animated feature film, like a modern big-studio CG movie. Exaggerated cartoon character designs, big expressive faces, smooth sculpted forms, soft warm subsurface shading, detailed fabric, hair and beard textures, warm saturated colors, soft global illumination. The rendering matches the reference images exactly and stays identical from the first frame to the last.

Camera language: real lenses between 28mm and 50mm, straight vertical lines, slow and smooth moves. No fisheye, no Dutch angles, no whip pans, no zooms, no shaky handheld.

Acting and movement: grounded and natural. The characters are designed like cartoons but move like real actors, at real human speed, with subtle facial expressions. Duane bawls, but moves like a real, heavy man: he rocks, he doesn't flail. Vince and Marco laugh like real people: shoulders shaking, eyes watering. Glenn doesn't move at all except for his breathing. No bouncing, no rubbery faces, no big gestures. Every movement is described exactly, including which hand does it: do exactly that and nothing extra.

Format: 20 seconds, 16:9, five shots joined by four hard cuts. Real-time motion at 24 fps. No slow motion, no speed ramps, no freeze frames.

Lighting: upstairs, the windowless hallway is almost completely black; only the whites of their eyes catch a tiny glimmer, plus one orange-white flash at 1.0s. In the living room (Shot 3) it's a sunny 2 PM: the sun through the half-closed blinds lays hard-edged, warm, striped bars of light across the couch and Glenn, with soft, slightly cooler shadow between them. The lamp stays off. At 11.0s the hallway's round ceiling light snaps back on at once: soft, even, warm light to the last frame. No flicker, no flashlights, no other light sources.

Physics: real weight and timing. Duane's huge body drops heavily, and the floor shakes with the thud. On the carpet he rocks from side to side on his back, knees bent up, and the caltrops in his socks clink. Glenn is completely relaxed; only his chest rises and falls with each snore. Faces and bodies keep their shape: no squash and stretch.

Sound: diegetic sound only. No music, no score. Clean dialogue with accurate lip sync whenever a mouth is visible, plus only these sounds, in order: nervous breathing in the dark; one deafening BANG echoing down the hallway; a split second of silence; Duane's sharp gasp; a heavy THUD and the clink of caltrops as he hits the carpet; Duane's voice with two censor beeps; Duane's long, wobbling bawl; in the living room, Glenn's deep, steady snore, a wall clock ticking out of frame, and the bawling, faint and muffled through the ceiling; upstairs, the bawling loud again; one distant CLACK from far below; the faint hum of the ceiling light coming on; two snorts of held-in laughter; laughter; Marco's voice with one censor beep; a pause; Vince's voice; louder laughter, with Duane still bawling under it. 12 PRINCIPLES OF ANIMATION (GROUNDED VERSION FOR THIS FILM)

1. Squash and stretch: none. Faces and bodies keep their shape.
2. Anticipation: small. Vince's eyes snap toward the sound before the flash, Duane's eyes go huge before they drop, and the two hold the laugh in before it breaks out.
3. Staging: one clear idea per shot: the flash, the scream, the sleeping dad, the reveal, the laugh.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: natural motion landing on clear, held poses: the frozen eyes, Duane clutching his shin, the two face to face.
5. Follow-through: subtle. Duane keeps rocking after the thud, and laughter keeps shaking their shoulders.
6. Slow in and slow out: every move eases at human speed; only the flash, the drop and the light coming on are instant.
7. Arcs: Duane rocks on a smooth arc, heads turn on slow arcs, and Vince's head falls back on a soft arc.
8. Secondary action: the clinking caltrops, the dust in the sunbeams and Vince's hand on his chest, never pulling focus.
9. Timing: a sudden flash, a fast, frantic scream, a still cutaway that holds long enough to land, then a slow build into the laugh.
10. Exaggeration: minimal. The comedy is Duane's outrage, two idiots who can't stop laughing, and Glenn sleeping through it all.
11. Solid drawing: consistent anatomy and volume in every frame, even in the dark; five fingers.
12. Appeal: three lovable idiots and one oblivious dad. It's a slapstick comedy.
