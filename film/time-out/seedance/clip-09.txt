SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: This continues straight on from the moment before, in the dim kitchen of the beige house. VINCE, MARCO and DUANE have just frozen mid-tiptoe because a voice said hi through the small white audio monitor on the counter. Vince decides it's nothing: he turns to the monitor, waves it off, and says it's probably just a recording. The monitor crackles, and BRAYDEN's voice answers him, by name. A long, silent pause. Vince slowly turns to Marco and asks how the voice knows his name. Marco doesn't answer right away. He takes out his pen gadget, breathes in through it, turns, and breathes a thick white cloud right into Vince's face. Then he points out, flatly, that Vince's name is painted on the side of their van. Through all of it, Duane keeps clinging to Marco's arm.

Continuity from the previous clip, true from the first frame: the three stand in single file in the middle of the kitchen floor, in their socks (Vince black socks with gold toes, Marco black socks, Duane white tube socks), with one foot still raised from the freeze. Duane's right hand grips Marco's left upper arm. Marco's pen gadget is tucked in his vest pocket. Brayden is never seen; his voice only comes out of the monitor's speaker.

Tone and pace: a dry, deadpan comedy. Slow and relaxed, with real pauses after every line. The long pause after the voice says Vince's name is the biggest beat: let it sit. Marco's answer is the flattest, slowest thing in the clip.

Look: stylized 3D animated feature film, like a modern big-studio CG movie. The rendering matches the reference images exactly and stays identical from the first frame to the last. Palette: a dim kitchen in cool shadow, pale yellow walls, a checkered floor, warm slivers of sunlight, and the monitor's three small green lights as the accent.

Camera language: real lenses between 35mm and 85mm, eye level, straight vertical lines, slow and smooth moves. No fisheye, no Dutch angles, no whip pans, no zooms, no shaky handheld.

Acting and movement: grounded and natural. The characters are designed like cartoons but move like real actors, at real human speed, with subtle facial expressions. Vince goes from confident to quietly rattled; his smile freezes rather than drops. Marco barely moves except for the pen gadget. Duane stays scared and still, holding on. Format: 20 seconds, 16:9, four shots joined by three hard cuts. Real-time motion at 24 fps. No slow motion, no speed ramps, no freeze frames.

Lighting: the kitchen is dim and quiet in the middle of a sunny afternoon. Every blind is shut, so thin slivers of warm sunlight leak through the slats and lie across the floor, the counter and the three of them. The monitor's three small green lights are the brightest points in the room. When Marco's cloud rolls through a sliver of sunlight, it lights up warm. The light is fixed to the world, not to the characters: when they move, the slivers of light slide across them. No flicker, no color shift.

Marco's cloud is thick white mist that comes out of his mouth only when he breathes out: it flows forward in a soft billow, wraps around Vince's head, hangs in the air, then drifts and thins over a few seconds. It never vanishes instantly and it never comes out of the pen. Duane's grip stays closed on Marco's sleeve the whole time. Faces and bodies keep their shape: no squash and stretch.

Sound: diegetic sound only. No music, no score. Clean dialogue with accurate lip sync, plus only these sounds, in order: the quiet hum of the fridge; Vince's voice; a crackle of static from the monitor; Brayden's voice through a small, tinny speaker; a long silence with only the fridge humming; Vince's voice with one censor beep; the soft rustle of Marco's vest pocket; a slow breath in; a long, soft breath out; one small cough from Vince; Marco's voice with one censor beep. The beeps are the only sounds that don't come from the scene itself.

12 PRINCIPLES OF ANIMATION (GROUNDED VERSION FOR THIS FILM)

1. Squash and stretch: none. Faces and bodies keep their shape.
2. Anticipation: small and natural. A breath before Vince turns, a crackle before the voice, a breath in before Marco's cloud.
3. Staging: one clear action per shot: the dismissal, the answer, the realization, the cloud.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: continuous, natural motion; during the long pause, everyone simply holds still.
5. Follow-through: subtle. The cloud keeps rolling and thinning after Marco stops breathing out, and Vince keeps blinking after it hits him.
6. Slow in and slow out: Vince's head turn and Marco's lean ease in and out at human speed.
7. Arcs: Vince's head turns on a smooth arc; the pen gadget travels on a short arc to Marco's mouth and back down.
8. Secondary action: dust in the slivers of light and the hum of the fridge, never pulling focus.
9. Timing: the long silence in Shot 3 is the biggest beat; Marco's answer is the slowest line.
10. Exaggeration: minimal. The comedy is Vince's frozen smile and Marco's total flatness.
11. Solid drawing: consistent anatomy and volume in every frame; five fingers.
12. Appeal: three lovable idiots, one of them slightly less of an idiot. It's a comedy.
