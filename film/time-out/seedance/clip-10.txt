SCENE CONTEXT & STYLE

What happens: Straight after the kitchen, DUANE tiptoes first into the long, dim downstairs hallway of the beige house, in his white tube socks. He doesn't see that the bare wooden floor is covered from wall to wall in small four-pointed steel caltrops. In slow motion, his socked foot comes down on one. His face goes through a slow series of expressions without a sound. Then he starts hopping on one foot down the hallway, and every hop lands on another caltrop, with one bleeped yell for each. He loses his balance and falls flat on his back, right on top of the rest of them. A red remote-control monster truck rolls out of the dark den doorway with a big plastic jar duct-taped to its back and a grabber claw on its front, and stops next to his face. BRAYDEN's voice comes from somewhere in the dark, counting his yells and charging him five dollars. Duane, crying, pulls five crumpled bills out of his fanny pack and feeds them into the jar one at a time. The truck backs away into the dark.

Continuity: Duane is in his white tube socks, with no shoes. Vince and Marco are still back in the kitchen and are not seen in this clip. Brayden is never seen: his voice comes from somewhere off-screen in the dark, and the truck is driven by remote control from off-screen.

Tone and pace: slapstick played completely straight, in a dry comedy. The slow-motion first step and Duane's silent face are long and quiet. The hopping and yelling are fast and rhythmic, one crunch and one beep per hop. Brayden's line and Duane paying up are slow, sad and dry.

Look: stylized 3D animated feature film, like a modern big-studio CG movie. Exaggerated cartoon character designs, big expressive faces, smooth sculpted forms, soft warm subsurface shading, detailed fabric, beard and wood textures, warm saturated colors, soft global illumination. The rendering matches the reference images exactly and stays identical from the first frame to the last. Palette: the dim honey-colored hallway, steel-grey caltrops, Duane's white socks, and the red truck as the accent.

Camera language: real lenses between 35mm and 85mm, straight vertical lines, slow and smooth moves. No fisheye, no Dutch angles, no whip pans, no zooms, no shaky handheld.

Acting and movement: grounded and natural. Duane is designed like a cartoon but moves like a real, very large man, with real weight. The pain is cartoon slapstick: big and funny, never graphic. His face changes slowly and clearly, not in a rubbery way. His hops are real one-footed hops with weight behind them. No bouncing, no rubbery faces, no stretching. Every movement is described exactly, including which hand or foot does it: do exactly that and nothing extra.

Format: 20 seconds, 16:9, five shots joined by four hard cuts. Shot 1 is in slow motion, and it is the only slow-motion shot. All the other shots are real-time at 24 fps. No speed ramps inside a shot, no freeze frames.

Lighting: the hallway is dim in the middle of a sunny afternoon. The ceiling light is off. Soft daylight leaks in from the kitchen doorway at the far end and from the den doorway on the right, so the hallway is mostly in shadow, with warm highlights on the floor. The steel points of the caltrops catch small glints. The truck's red paint catches the light when it rolls out. The light is fixed to the world, not to the characters. No flicker, no color shift.

Physics: real weight and gravity. Each caltrop always has one point sticking straight up. When Duane steps on one, it stays in place under his sock. His hops have real weight, and each landing makes the floor thud and the nearby caltrops jump and rattle. When he falls, he goes down backward in one heavy movement onto the floor, and the caltrops around him scatter and clatter. The truck is a toy with real weight: it rolls on its knobby tires, stops with a small rock, and backs up in a straight line. The bills are soft, crumpled paper and drop into the jar. Nothing graphic is ever shown. Faces and bodies keep their shape: no squash and stretch.

Sound: diegetic sound only. No music, no score. Clean dialogue with accurate lip sync, plus only these sounds, in order: in the slow-motion shot, a low, slowed-down hush, then one loud, sharp CRUNCH as the foot lands; silence while Duane's face changes; one CRUNCH under each hop, each with his yell and a censor beep; a heavy thud and a scattering metallic clatter as he lands on his back; the thin electric whine of the truck's motor and its tires on wood; Brayden's voice from off-screen, echoing slightly down the hallway; Duane sniffling; the zip of his fanny pack; five soft rustles of paper going into the jar; the motor whining in reverse as the truck backs away. Five words are covered by a TV censor beep. Wherever a line says [BLEEP] or [BLEEEEP], a short, clean censor beep plays instead of that word ([BLEEEEP] is longer), and Duane's mouth keeps moving under it. The beeps are the only sounds that don't come from the scene itself.

12 PRINCIPLES OF ANIMATION (GROUNDED VERSION FOR THIS FILM)

1. Squash and stretch: none on faces or bodies. His face changes through clear expressions without stretching.
2. Anticipation: the slow-motion foot coming down is the setup; each hop pushes off before it lands; his arms windmill before he falls.
3. Staging: one clear action per shot: the step, the face, the hops, the truck, paying up.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: each of the five expressions in Shot 2 is a clear held pose with a smooth change between them.
5. Follow-through: the caltrops jump and rattle after each landing and scatter after the fall; the truck rocks once when it stops.
6. Slow in and slow out: the slow-motion descent eases down; the dolly and the push-ins ease in and out.
7. Arcs: his hops and his fall backward follow natural, heavy arcs.
8. Secondary action: glints on the caltrops and the sniffles and the trembling chin while he pays, never pulling focus.
9. Timing: slow and silent at the start, fast and rhythmic in the hops, slow and sad at the end.
10. Exaggeration: kept to the slapstick itself. His face is expressive but never rubbery, and the comedy is the rhythm of crunch, yell, beep.
11. Solid drawing: a huge man with real weight and consistent volume in every frame; five fingers.
12. Appeal: a big, sweet guy having the worst day of his life. It's a comedy.
