# TIME OUT: Scene & Shot Breakdown

63 shots · 11 scenes · **3:58 of picture + a few seconds of credits = about 4:00**

## How to read each shot

- **Image** is the prompt for the **start frame**. Generate it as an image with that shot's character and set references attached.
- **Motion** is the prompt for **image-to-video**. It describes only what moves and what the camera does. Don't re-describe the look.
- **Dialogue** is the spoken lines. If you use a video model with native audio, add `CHARACTER says: "line"` to the Motion prompt. Otherwise record or generate the VO separately and lip-sync it.
- **Audio** is the music cues (see `characters.md`) and sound effects.
- **Post** is anything to add in the edit, mostly on-screen text.
- **Durations** are *edit* lengths. Generate at your model's minimum clip length (usually 5s) and trim.

**Wherever a name appears in CAPS** (GLENN, BRAYDEN, VINCE, MARCO, DUANE, OFFICER DALE), attach that character's reference sheet. If your model doesn't take references, paste their **look tag** from `characters.md`. From Scene 3 on, use Brayden's **war-mode** sheet.

**Always add the style block** from `characters.md` to the end of every prompt.

**The swearing is uncensored. This is 18+ and nothing gets bleeped.** Some AI video tools refuse prompts with strong language. If one does, leave that line out of the video prompt, generate the voice separately (voice generators are usually fine with it), and lip-sync it.

**Weapons and the kid.** Most AI video tools block a child and a weapon in the same frame. That's why every knife, gun and spike shot here is a close-up or a trap firing on its own, with Brayden in a separate shot. Edit them back to back and it reads the same. If a shot still gets blocked, call the item a "cartoon prop" (e.g. "cartoon prop knives") or describe Brayden as "a small cartoon toddler character."
