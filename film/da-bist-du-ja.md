# DA BIST DU JA
### *There You Are*

An animated short in the style of Vincent van Gogh
Runtime ≈ 2:15 · One line of dialogue, spoken three times

> **Logline:** One star slips out of *The Starry Night* to live a single human life. The sky keeps her place.

## The rules of this world

- **She is yellow. He is blue.** Where they touch, the paint turns green, so their child has green eyes.
- **Color follows love.** When love is near, the paint is thick and blazing. When love is lost, it thins into the browns and grays of Van Gogh's early, darker years.
- **No cuts.** Every transition happens inside the paint: a swirl becomes a swirl, a reaching hand becomes a reaching hand, a rocking cradle becomes a rocking boat.
- **Nothing is ever still.** Every brushstroke keeps moving, even in the quietest frame.
- **One line, one song.** The only words are „Da bist du ja" (*There you are*), spoken by three generations. The only melody is the old German lullaby „Weißt du, wie viel Sternlein stehen", about the stars in the blue sky, every one of them counted, *„dass ihm auch nicht eines fehlet"* (so that not a single one is missing).

## 1 · THE MISSING STAR (*before*)
**0:00–0:09** · *Painted after: The Starry Night (1889)*

Open on *The Starry Night*, exactly as it hangs in New York. Still. Silent.

Then the wind rises, and the painting starts to breathe: the great swirl turns, the cypress flickers like black fire, every star pulses in rings of light.

One small star beside the swirl trembles. It looks down at the sleeping village, at one window glowing gold, and lets go.

It falls as a streak of yellow paint. Its halo stays behind in the sky: **a ring of light circling nothing.**

→ We fall with it, past the cypress and the church spire, straight toward that window.

## 2 · ALMOND BLOSSOM (*birth*)
**0:09–0:19** · *Painted after: Almond Blossom (1890), which Van Gogh made to celebrate the birth of his nephew*

The star slips through the glass and curls into the tiny whorl of hair on a newborn's crown: the sky's swirl, in miniature.

Her first cry, and the room bursts into bloom. White almond blossoms crack open across a turquoise sky, branch by branch, stroke by stroke.

Her mother, exhausted, laughing and crying at once, presses her lips to the whorl.

**MOTHER** *(whispering)*: „Da bist du ja." *(There you are.)*

She hums the first notes of the lullaby.

## 3 · FIRST STEPS (*childhood*)
**0:19–0:30** · *Painted after: First Steps, after Millet (1890)*

A cottage garden at golden hour. Her father drops his spade and kneels, arms wide open. Her mother holds the toddler upright (yellow dress, gold curls) and lets go.

One step. Two. Three. She tumbles into his arms.

He swings her up toward the sky. As she rises, the evening turns deep blue and the first stars come out. Up there, above his laughter, she sees it: the empty ring of light.

She stares at it the way you stare at a word you almost remember. She stretches her tiny hand toward it.

She can't reach.

→ Her reaching hand grows, stroke by stroke, into the hand of a young woman…

## 4 · CAFÉ TERRACE AT NIGHT (*love*)
**0:30–0:42** · *Painted after: Café Terrace at Night (1888)*

…still raised toward the stars, twenty years later, on a crowded café terrace.

A young man in a blue fisherman's jacket thinks she's asking him to dance. He takes her hand.

She turns, startled. He's mortified. She doesn't let go.

Inside, a violin turns the lullaby into a waltz. They dance across the cobblestones, and **where yellow touches blue, the paint between their fingers turns green.**

Next to his blue, her yellow burns brighter. Above them the stars begin to turn, and the great swirl spins with them. The whole sky is dancing.

## 5 · LA BERCEUSE (*a child*)
**0:42–0:51** · *Painted after: La Berceuse (1889), a mother holding the rope of a cradle*

Their little house. Green wallpaper, with flowers that open as she sings.

She sits holding the rope of a cradle, rocking. Inside, a newborn with a swirl of hair on its crown opens its eyes. Green eyes: her yellow and his blue.

She leans in and kisses the whorl.

**SHE:** „Da bist du ja."

In the doorway, he wipes his eyes on his blue sleeve. She hums the lullaby. Now it's hers to sing.

## 6 · THE STORM (*the night she almost lost him*)
**0:51–1:02** · *Painted after: Seascape near Les Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer (1888), and La Berceuse, which Van Gogh imagined hanging in a fishing boat's cabin so sailors would remember their own lullabies*

Night. Rain lashes the window. He's out at sea.

The sea is paint thrown in fury: thick green-white impasto, waves like knife-cut mountains. His small boat is tossed like a leaf.

At home she lights a lamp and sets it in the window. She keeps rocking the cradle…

→ …and the rocking cradle becomes the rocking boat. Same rhythm. Same lullaby, now fighting the thunder.

In the black, he sees one tiny point of yellow on the shore. Her lamp. He turns the rudder toward it.

Dawn. The door bangs open. He's soaked. He's alive. She's in his arms before he's through the door.

From that night on, the lamp burns in the window every night.

## 7 · NOON (*a lifetime, and a goodbye*)
**1:02–1:15** · *Painted after: Noon – Rest from Work, after Millet (1890)*

A haystack in a blazing wheat field under a cobalt sky. The two of them asleep in its shade: shoes kicked off, his straw hat over his eyes, her head on his shoulder, fingers laced.

The camera never moves. The world does.

Wheat rises and falls in waves of brushstrokes. Snow. Blossom. Harvest. Behind them, their child grows up in three strokes: crawling, running, then walking away down the road with a suitcase, turning once to wave.

Their fingers tighten.

Their hair turns white. Their hands grow old. They never let go.

Summer again. She wakes. Stretches. Nudges him.

He doesn't move.

She lifts the straw hat, and just holds it.

Slowly, all the blue drains out of the painting (out of the sky, out of the shadows, out of everything) and rises.

## 8 · THE EMPTY CHAIR (*grief*)
**1:15–1:27** · *Painted after: Van Gogh's Chair (1888), in the dark browns of The Potato Eaters (1885)*

The house is painted in mud and ash now. The strokes are thin and dry, like a brush running out of paint. No blue anywhere except his coat, which she wears, the sleeves hanging past her hands.

His chair stands empty. His pipe still lies on the seat.

Every night she still lights the lamp and sets it in the window. For someone who isn't coming home.

She looks up. The empty ring in the sky has changed. It glows now with a deep, living blue, pulsing softly. Like someone waiting at her place.

She presses her palm to the cold glass.

## 9 · SUNFLOWERS (*a reason*)
**1:27–1:40** · *Painted after: Sunflowers (1888), one vase holding every stage of a flower's life, from full bloom to wilting*

A knock. Her grown child, green-eyed, and peeking out from behind a leg, a little granddaughter.

The girl marches straight past everyone, climbs into the empty chair, jams the pipe in her mouth and puffs out her cheeks into a perfect Grandpa face.

A beat.

And the old woman laughs, for the first time since.

Color floods back into the room like a sunrise. The wilted sunflowers on the table lift their heads and blaze, the floor flushes red, the walls turn blue again.

Every night that summer, at the window, she teaches the girl the lullaby, pointing up at the blue ring in the sky. The girl gets half the words wrong. Grandma kisses the swirl on her crown.

## 10 · LAST STEPS (*home*)
**1:40–2:10** · *Painted after: Wheatfield with a Reaper (1889) → First Steps → The Starry Night*

Night. The girl has fallen asleep in Grandpa's chair. Grandma tucks the blue coat around her, kisses the whorl, and leaves the lamp burning.

She opens the door,

and outside, it isn't night. It's a wheat field at noon, flooded with gold. *(Van Gogh, on painting death: "there's nothing sad in this death… a sun flooding everything with a light of pure gold.")*

Across the field, a figure made of blue brushstrokes kneels with his arms wide open, exactly the way her father did.

Him. Twenty again.

She steps out. Old. Unsteady. One step. Two. With every step the paint of her face re-strokes itself younger: white hair turns gold, her back straightens. She walks. Then she runs, like a girl through the wheat.

She falls into his arms. His lips move. We don't hear the words. We don't need to.

He lifts her high, the way her father did. He spins her, the way he did at the café. The spin carries them up: the field tilts into the sky, the gold breaks into stars, and they rise, turning, through the great swirl.

He sets her gently into the empty ring.

She fits.

She ignites. His blue closes around her like a halo.

*(Sound: a single brushstroke laid onto canvas. Then silence.)*

Far below, in the village, one window is lit, like the one she fell toward a lifetime ago.

The girl is awake, wrapped in the blue coat, holding the yellow lamp. Blue and yellow, both of them, in one small child. She searches the sky, finds the new star in the old empty place, and smiles.

**GIRL:** „Da bist du ja, Oma." *(There you are, Grandma.)*

She starts to sing, softly, alone, and this time she gets every word right:
*„Weißt du, wie viel Sternlein stehen…"*

The camera pulls back: the window, the rooftops, the church, the cypress, the hills, the swirling sky. Until we're looking at *The Starry Night*, exactly as it hangs in New York.

Complete.

## END CARD
**2:10–2:17** · White letters on deep blue

> „Es gibt kein Blau ohne Gelb."
> *There is no blue without yellow.*
> — Vincent van Gogh, 1888

Fade in: **DA BIST DU JA**

## Notes for production

- **Need it at 2:00?** Cut Scene 6 and have her set the lamp in the window in Scene 5, waiting for him to come home from the sea. Nothing else can go, because every other scene sets up something the ending pays off.
- **Music:** the lullaby is public domain (text by Wilhelm Hey, published 1837; traditional melody), but existing recordings aren't, so record your own. Let it travel through the film: hummed at the birth → music box at the first steps → waltz at the café → hummed at the cradle → strings fighting the storm → solo piano at noon → **total silence in the grief scene** → the child's off-key singing brings it back → full orchestra on the final pull-back.
- **Sound:** build the foley from real brush-on-canvas recordings. Wind in the wheat is a hundred brushes; the storm is a palette knife. Save the single cleanest brushstroke for the moment she becomes a star.
- **The quote:** from Van Gogh's letter to Émile Bernard, June 1888. The full line is *"There is no blue without yellow and without orange."* If you want all of it on the card („Es gibt kein Blau ohne Gelb und ohne Orange."), the orange is already in the film: the lamp's flame in the girl's hands.
