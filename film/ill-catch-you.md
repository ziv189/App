# I'LL CATCH YOU
*Ich fang dich auf*

A wordless animated short · Van Gogh-style world, Picasso-style characters · about 2:40

**Logline:** Two souls play in the sky before they're born. One of them has to go first.

**The idea:** The people who love us go first, into the world and out of it, so they can be there to catch us. One day, we go first for someone else.

## How this world works

- **Souls are light. People are Picasso.** Before birth and after death, souls are small lights in a swirling Van Gogh sky. In life they become Picasso-style people: faces built from flat, colored planes, seen from several sides at once.
- **You get your face by being seen.** A newborn's face is a blank oval until someone looks at it with love. Under that gaze it builds itself, plane by plane. People nobody really looks at go flat: gray paper profiles with only one side.
- **Love sees every side and every age at once.** That's why the faces are cubist. When someone looks at a person they love, we see that person the way love does.
- **The world feels what they feel.** The brushstrokes swirl faster with joy and freeze in grief. Warm Rose Period tones for love, Blue Period blue for loss.
- **One shape, one song.** The shape is a crescent curled around a dot, like a mother's arm around a baby's head. The song is five wordless notes that fall and then lift on the last one: the sound of being caught.

## ACT ONE · THE SKY
*No bodies, no faces, no time. Wonder, play, and the first goodbye.*

### 1 · TWO LIGHTS (0:00–0:14)

Black. One brushstroke of light curls out of the dark. Then another. Then thousands, until a whole night sky is turning: rivers of blue and gold paint, swirling like galaxies.

Two small lights are playing in it. One is amber, shaped like a crescent, bold, throwing sparks. The other is tiny and pale, flickering like a candle in a draft.

The amber light surfs a swirl like a wave. The little light tries, and tumbles off. The amber light loops back, nudges it onto the current, and they ride it together, faster and faster. Every time the little light laughs, it flares brighter.

Tired, they drift into the calm eye of a swirl and curl up together: the crescent around the dot.

Far below, through a gap in the clouds, lies a world of wheat fields, cypress trees and villages with lit windows.

### 2 · GOING FIRST (0:14–0:28)

The calm eye opens beneath them into a whirlpool, a current spiraling down toward the world. It's pulling at the amber light. Its time has come.

The little light panics and wraps itself around the amber one.

The amber light unwinds it, gently. Touches its crescent to the little one's glow. Hums five notes: falling, falling, then lifting at the end.

Then it lets go: a streak of amber, shrinking toward the world.

The little light is alone. The sky is suddenly enormous, and the swirls feel like a storm. It hides in the halo of a star while the heavens wheel past, and it grows dim.

Then the current comes for it. It fights, trembling. It looks down through the whirlpool, and far, far below, one window is glowing amber. Faintly, from somewhere down there: five notes, falling, then lifting.

It stops trembling.

It lets go.

### 3 · THE CATCH (0:28–0:43)

The camera falls with it through every layer of the painted sky: night blue, dawn violet, morning gold. Through clouds of thick paint and flocks of brushstroke birds, faster and faster, toward that window,

and with a newborn's first cry, it lands in a pair of arms.

A young mother, drawn like a Picasso: a face of warm rose planes, one eye seen from the front, one in profile. On her cheek, one plane glows amber, in the shape of a crescent.

Close on the baby. Her face is blank: a smooth, unfinished oval. Her mother looks at her, and wherever her gaze lands, the face builds itself. An eye. A second eye, from another angle. A nose in profile. A mouth. The baby gets her face by being seen.

The new eyes open and find the amber crescent on her mother's cheek. A tiny hand reaches for it.

Her mother hums five notes, falling, then lifting, and curls her arm around the baby's head.

The crescent around the dot.

She went first, so she could catch her.

## ACT TWO · THE WORLD
*Faces, seen and unseen. Warmth, rebellion, loneliness, love, and the hardest goodbye.*

### 4 · JUMP (0:43–0:52)

A garden wall at the edge of a swirling wheat field. A toddler wobbles along the top. Below, her mother opens her arms.

She jumps. Caught, and spun around, the wheat spinning with them.

Again, older. Caught. Again, legs longer now. Caught. Every jump, the arms are there. Every catch, the whole field swirls with joy.

Through the girl's eyes, her mother is simple: warm, round, just Mom. Through her mother's eyes, the girl is a dazzle of facets, every side of her at once.

### 5 · THE WRONG SIDE OF THE WALL (0:52–1:04)

The same wall, years later. The girl is a teenager, and her face is at its most Picasso: sharp angles, planes that don't agree with each other. A packed bag on her shoulder.

Below, her mother opens her arms, the way she always has.

The girl looks at them. Then she jumps off the other side of the wall, onto the road, alone. She lands hard, gets up, and walks away down a road that twists off into the distance.

She looks back once. Through her eyes, her mother is flat: a gray paper silhouette in the doorway. One profile. No depth at all.

We stay on the mother. Her arms stay open long after the girl is gone. Then, slowly, they come down.

### 6 · THE FLAT CITY (1:04–1:21)

A city at night. Streets running with swirling lamplight, rain slanting down in strokes. Crowds rush past, and every person is flat: gray paper profiles sliding past each other like shadows. Nobody looks at anybody.

She walks among them, and she's flattening too. Her planes fold in. Her color drains. One more gray profile.

Someone collides with her, and her things scatter across the wet street. He kneels to gather them, another flat gray shape, glances up, and actually looks at her.

Under his gaze she unfolds: color, depth, every side of her opening at once. She looks back at him, and he unfolds too. Two people in full color in a city of shadows.

Rain turns to blossom, to snow, to blossom again. Then one night a small pale light falls out of the sky, into her arms. A baby with a blank, unfinished face. She looks at it, and her gaze builds that face, plane by plane.

Without meaning to, she hums five notes, falling, then lifting.

She stops. She knows this song.

She looks out the window, at the long road home.

### 7 · ALL OF HER (1:21–1:38)

The old garden. The wall looks so small now. She stands at the door with her baby in her arms.

Over her shoulder, the door opens. Her mother is old.

And for the first time, the daughter sees her whole. Her mother's face unfolds into every side and every age at once: the young woman who caught her, the mother spinning in the wheat, a little girl the daughter never knew, the gray silhouette in the doorway, full of depth now. And at the center of it all, glowing on her cheek, the amber crescent.

For one heartbeat, the daughter's eyes fill with a swirling sky and two lights playing in it.

She remembers.

Her face breaks. Her mother opens her arms.

She falls into them.

Caught.

### 8 · GOING FIRST, AGAIN (1:38–1:53)

Now the daughter does the catching. An elbow on the garden step. A stumble in the hallway. At night she carries her mother to the window to look at the sky.

One night at that window, her mother touches her cheek and hums the five notes. Falling. Falling…

The last note never comes.

The amber light lifts out of her and drifts up through the window into the swirling sky. The daughter reaches for it. Her hands close on air.

Then every brushstroke in the world stops. The wheat freezes mid-wave. The sky freezes mid-swirl. Everything sinks into blue, a whole world in mourning, the color of Picasso's Blue Period.

Silence.

---

## ACT THREE · ALL AT ONCE
*A whole life seen at once. Grief turns into courage, and the end becomes a beginning.*

### 9 · JUMP, AGAIN (1:53–2:06)

A small hand tugs at hers: her own child, humming the five notes all wrong.

She laughs through her tears, and the world starts moving again. The swirls turn. The color comes back.

Time pours past like wind through wheat. Her hair turns white.

The garden wall. A grandchild wobbles along the top. Below, an old woman opens her arms.

The child jumps. Caught.

Her family looks at her, and we see her the way they do, every age at once: the pale little light, the blank-faced baby, the girl on the wall, the teenager on the wrong side of it, the gray shadow in the flat city, the young mother, the daughter at the door, the old woman. And on her cheek, faint but glowing, a small amber crescent.

She looks up at the night sky.

She isn't afraid.

### 10 · I'LL CATCH YOU (2:06–2:32)

Her light lifts out of her and rises through the window, falling upward into the sky.

For a moment she's that tiny, pale, flickering thing again, tumbling through a sky far too big for her,

and she's caught.

Amber light curls around her. The crescent around the dot.

And the last note finally sounds. Falling, falling, and at last, lifting.

They spin. They surf the swirls the way they used to, faster and faster, and every time she laughs, she flares brighter.

Then, from behind a swirl, a tiny pale light peeks out. New. Trembling. Staring down at the world below.

She looks at her mother. Her mother smiles.

She drifts over to the little one, her own light warm now, edged with amber. She nudges it onto a current and loops back for it when it tumbles, and they ride together.

The last shot is the first shot: two lights chasing each other through the swirling sky, the bigger one a little ahead, turning back to wait.

The bigger one is her now.

### END CARD (2:32–2:40)

The crescent and the dot, drawn in one brushstroke of light. The five notes play once more and end on the lift.

Then the title, handwritten in many languages at once, swirling up into the night like brushstrokes:

*I'll catch you · Ich fang dich auf · Je te rattrape · Ti prendo io · Yo te atrapo · Eu te pego · Ik vang je op · Złapię cię · Я тебя поймаю · אני אתפוס אותך · 我会接住你 · 受け止めるよ · 내가 잡아줄게*

## Notes

- **No dialogue anywhere,** so the film plays the same in every country. The only words are the end title. Have a native speaker handwrite each language, and add as many as you like.
- **The five notes carry the story.** You hear them in the sky, at the birth, from the daughter, and from her child. From scene 8 on, hold back the last note (even the child's off-key version shouldn't land it) until the catch in scene 10.
- **The face-building idea echoes real psychology.** The psychoanalyst D. W. Winnicott wrote that when a baby looks at its mother's face, what it sees is itself.
- **If you need it shorter,** trim inside scenes 4 and 9 (the jump montages). Every other scene sets up something the ending pays off.
