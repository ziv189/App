SCENE CONTEXT
The opening of a hand-painted 2D animated film, before anyone is born: only a night sky, no ground, no people. Out of pure black the sky paints itself into a great swirl. Two small glowing lights play in it: the bold amber light rides the upper band; the little light follows, wobbles and tumbles off into the calm dark; the amber light comes back, lifts it up, and they ride together, faster and faster, the little one flaring twice with joy. Tired, they drift into the calm center of the swirl and curl up: the amber light's crescent shape around the little light, the key shape of the film. Then the camera tilts down through the clouds to the world below, where one cottage window glows amber. One continuous shot, 15 seconds, no cuts. Wonder, then joy, then peace.

STYLE
Hand-painted 2D animation in the style of Vincent van Gogh's late landscapes and night skies: thick impasto oil paint, long rhythmic curved brushstrokes that follow every form, a sky built from swirling curved dashes, cobalt against chrome yellow. Clear, readable shapes: the strokes describe the forms instead of burying them in noise. No smooth gradients, no photographic detail. The strokes are always gently moving, streaming along their curves like slow-flowing paint; every stroke keeps its painted edges and never melts, blurs or smears. All the texture lives in the sky and the world; the lights stay simple. The lights are small glowing points painted in thick luminous brushstrokes, like stars in a Van Gogh night sky: a bright core with a soft ring of short curved paint dabs around it. Painted light, not a lens flare and not a CGI glow. The lights have no faces and no bodies.

PHYSICS
Strokes stream along the bands while the great swirl turns slowly clockwise; the calm dark space and the calm center stay smooth and almost still. The lights are weightless, carried by the flow of the strokes: faster on a band, slower off it. The tumble is soft, sinking slowly as if through thick air: never a crash or a bounce. The clouds are thick, slow and soft and part by themselves like heavy paint, never like smoke. Nothing burns, breaks, splashes or explodes.

ANIMATION PRINCIPLES
Anticipation: the little light shrinks before it hops; the amber light dips under it before the nudge. Arcs: every stroke and both lights move on curves, never straight lines. Squash and stretch: gentle, on the little light only; the amber light never changes shape. Follow-through: trails of dabs lag behind every turn; rings settle a moment after each stop. Slow in and slow out on every move. Secondary action: the sky's strokes answer every feeling. Timing: sparse at the start, building to joy, then a long calm hold. Staging: one clear action at a time. Appeal: playful, warm and tender.

LIGHTING (NIGHT)
Night. The only light comes from the stars and the glowing lights: cool cobalt shadows, warm gold highlights.
The sky's own light is fixed for the whole shot: gold strokes carry the warm highlights, cobalt and midnight dashes the cool shadows.
The two lights are the only moving light sources: the amber light throws a warm amber-gold glow on the strokes about one light-width around it, the little light a fainter pale blue-white glow. Their glow travels with them across the strokes.
No lens flare, no bloom haze, no volumetric beams, no glitter particles.
Brightness order: the amber light brightest; the little light next, matching it only at the instant of each flare; then the gold strokes; the stars small and steady. At rest both lights dim and the strokes around them cool slightly.
Below, the other windows glow only faintly beside the amber one; the cloud ring stays deep cobalt.
No moon, no sun, no lamp, no second light source, no color shift, no strobing; the only flicker is the little light's own when it is scared.
