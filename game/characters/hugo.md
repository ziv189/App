# Old Hugo (`hugo`)
- **Name:** Old Hugo. **Age:** about 70 (the bible only says "old").
- **Role:** Toymaker and automaton-mender in Vermeil. Knew Elias from the clock workshops, when they were young.
- **Logline:** A grumpy toymaker who still respects Élias and refuses to believe the stopped city is a lost cause.

**Look:** Short and a little hunched, big grey bushy beard, round wire spectacles on the nose, blue work smock with a pencil in the pocket, flat cap, magnifying loupe in one hand.
**Palette:** smock #3c5e8b · beard #b8b2a8 · cap #4a3b2a · brass #c9963f · skin #e2b08c · pencil #e8b84d.
**Movement:** slow, heavy, stubborn. Short steps, little arm swing, a head that stays put. Lifts the loupe to one eye every few seconds.

**Personality:** blunt, dry, black humour, hates fuss and hurry, judges people by their hands. **Fears:** his toys outliving the people who loved them; Élias being truly lost. **Wants:** to be left alone with his work, and proved wrong about the stopped hour.

**Voice:**
- "Put that down. It's older than your manners and it bites."
- "Élias could fix anything with a spring and a bad mood. Never once saw him stop a clock."
- "Nobody gets a minute back for free, boy. Not even Élias."

**Relationships:**
- **Léo:** sees a boy carrying a broken spring. Gruff, then quietly generous.
- **Élias:** old friend and rival; respects him more than he will say.
- **Juliette:** he promised her a wooden horse ten years ago and never finished it.
- **Mireille:** tolerates her thieving and trades insults. Pays her in gossip.
- **Madame Pivert, Bastien, Gaspard:** neighbours he grumbles at and secretly watches out for.

**Arc across the chapters:**
- **Ch1, Vermeil Station:** offstage. His name is stamped on a crate of wind-up birds on the platform, and Léo's pocket watch ticks harder when he reads it.
- **Ch2, The Frozen Market:** met at his frozen stall. Refuses to help until Léo proves he is not a thief. Gives the hint that Élias came to him on the night the clock stopped. Lends Léo his loupe.
- **Ch3, The Foundries:** offstage. Léo recalls his advice that a sleeping automaton should be woken gently, which he applies to Gaspard.
- **Ch4, Inside the Tower:** offstage. In the gears, Léo finds the small broken spring by looking closely through the loupe, which is Hugo's lesson ("the broken part is always the small one").
- **Ch5, The Dial:** not at the summit. The unfinished wooden horse goes to Juliette in the good ending, and stays unfinished in the bad one.
