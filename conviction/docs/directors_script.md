# CONVICTION: Director's Script

Sep 27, 2026 · @Ziv

The complete game, scene by scene, from the first nightmare to the last replay. Stage directions describe what the player sees and hears; GAMEPLAY marks where the player has control.

## The look and the sound

The game has three worlds, and each one is lit, framed and scored differently. The player should know which world they're in within one second of any cut.

### The three worlds

**The day (investigation).** January, a rain-soaked American city, around 2011. Desaturated color, sodium-yellow streetlights, cold blue shadows, visible breath, heavy film grain. Interiors are dim, and Ward's flashlight is the key light in every crime scene.

**The nightmare.** The same rooms, remembered wrong. Walls breathe slowly, ceilings sit too low, and every room stretches lengthwise toward its front door. A red glow with no source fills the room, and the only cool light is a thin line of hallway light under the door behind Lily. It's always silent outside: no rain, no traffic, because memory doesn't record weather.

**The Room (the frame).** One desk lamp clamped to a filing cabinet behind Ward, aimed at the glass. Everything else is black. A 60-cycle hum never stops.

### Camera rules


- In the Room, never show Ward's hands. Frame him chest-up, over the shoulder toward the glass, or low behind the table edge. The first time the camera tilts down to his hands is the reveal.
- Never show Ward's reflection, anywhere. There are no mirrors in the game. Windows at night reflect the room, never Ward. In his bathroom, four screw holes and a pale rectangle of paint mark where a mirror used to hang.
- Warm light appears exactly once: when the lights come up on the other side of the glass in the finale. It should feel like the first real light in the game.
- Ward wakes every morning on the floor, one arm stretched toward the front door. It's the same pose as every body the player finds.

### Sound motifs


- **The three knocks.** Soft, evenly spaced, like someone trying not to wake the building. Walt's morning knock, the radiator, the dryers, the basement pipes and, finally, Lily. Every knock in the game uses this exact rhythm.
- **The pin.** Pinning a suspect makes a heavy, too-loud thunk. The same thunk plays faintly under every phone call that announces a death.
- **The hum.** The Room's lamp hum is buried under every nightmare's ambience, because every nightmare is being told from that room.
- **The clock.** Each nightmare has a clock whose second hand ticks but never moves forward.
- **The monster's roar.** A human voice begging, pitched down two octaves and distorted. Pitched back up, it says "please."
- **Lily's whisper.** Her final line from the finale, played in reverse. Players who reverse it early will hear a name.

### Horror rules


1. Doors are the threat. Every scare comes from a door: knocking, opening, being blocked, being reached for.
2. Silence beats noise. The holding cell and the basement are the quietest moments in the game, and the most frightening.
3. Nothing in the daytime ever attacks Ward. The horror is what the player does, not what's done to them.
4. Wrongness beats jump scares. Every morning, one small thing in the apartment is wrong. The game never points at it.
5. Subliminal frames. During every boss fight, each successful hit cuts in a single frame of a real, terrified human face caught in a flashlight beam. Most players won't consciously see it.

### The flashlight

Ward's long, heavy metal flashlight is the player's investigation tool and the nightmare weapon. Examined in the inventory, it gains a new dent near the lens after every murder night. Ward always has an explanation.

### Gameplay key


- **Investigation:** explore, examine objects, interview witnesses. Ward's voice-over comments on what the player examines.
- **The board:** arrange evidence under each suspect. Once a suspect's Motive, Opportunity and Means are all filled, the player can PIN them. Whoever is pinned dies that night. Every board holds two or three credible suspects, but the evidence completes only one case. Each night the player can pin that suspect, refuse to pin (see Alternate beat), or complete a case against Ward himself (see Secret ending).
- **Nightmares:** LIGHT (hold) forces the monster to shield itself and stagger back. STRIKE attacks. DODGE evades. A bar labeled PROTECT HER drains whenever the monster reaches the door.

## Prologue

### 1. INT. THE ROOM — NIGHT

Black. A low electrical hum fades up. A lamp clicks on behind someone, and the glare hits a wall of dark glass.

ELIAS WARD, 50s, sits at a steel table in shirtsleeves. Unshaven, eyes red, collar open. No tie. He faces a one-way glass the size of a wall. The lamp behind him turns the glass into a black mirror full of glare.

On the other side of the glass, in the dark, sits a SILHOUETTE. A man's shape, seated, shoulders slightly hunched. No face. No movement.

A speaker crackles in the ceiling. The VOICE is distorted, low, impossible to place.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Tell us about the killer, detective.

Ward looks at the silhouette for a long moment. Then he smiles, tired.

**WARD:** With him sitting right there?

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** He's not going anywhere.

**WARD:** No. He's not.

He leans forward. In the background of the shot, soft and out of focus, the silhouette leans forward too.

**WARD:** Where do you want me to start?

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Where it started.

**WARD:** It started the night Victor Hale died. I didn't know it yet. I was asleep.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Asleep.

**WARD:** Out cold, on my floor. Same as every night that winter.

The lamp flickers. The hum drops half a tone.

_Director's note: A low shot under the table catches Ward's shoes for one second. They have no laces. Detainees have their laces, belts and ties taken at booking; players will read it as a man who stopped caring. Behind Ward is a steel door with no handle on this side. Whenever Ward moves in this room, something metal clinks faintly below the frame. Never comment on any of it._

CUT TO BLACK.

### 2. NIGHTMARE — THE COLD OPEN

Total darkness. Then, floating in it, a clock face: **3:40.** The second hand ticks, but never moves forward.

A thin line of cold light appears low in the dark, like light under a door. A shape stands in front of it: a GIRL, fifteen, in a faded green cardigan too big for her. Her face is in shadow. She's whispering. We can't make out a word.

Behind the camera, something breathes. Wet, slow, too close.

**GAMEPLAY:** The player has control for the first time. The only prompt on screen: LIGHT. A flashlight clicks on in the player's hand.

The beam swings and finds it: a TALL SHAPE, stooped, faceless, its skin like wet bedsheets, its arms too long. In the beam it throws its long hands up over where its face should be and staggers back. It roars: low, grinding, distorted.

Then it lunges past the player toward the girl and the door behind her.

**GAMEPLAY:** Prompts appear: LIGHT (hold). STRIKE. The player blocks it, drives it back with the beam, strikes three times. On each hit, a single frame flashes: a man's face, eyes wide, caught in a flashlight beam. Too fast to register.

The shape collapses at the girl's feet. One long arm stretches past her ankles toward the door. Its fingers scrape the floorboards and stop.

The girl looks down at it. Then up at the player. Her lips move. The line of light under the door goes out.

Silence. Then three soft knocks, evenly spaced.

CUT TO:

### 3. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — MORNING

Gray light through rain-streaked windows. A small, tired apartment on the fourth floor of an old brick walk-up: an armchair facing the front door, a desk, a radiator ticking, empty bottles.

Ward lies face down on the floor between the armchair and the front door. One arm is stretched toward the door.

The wall phone rings. And rings. Ward stirs, pushes himself up. His long coat lies crumpled by the door, damp at the hem. The door chain hangs loose.

He answers.

**RUTH (ON PHONE):** Sir? It's Adler.

**WARD:** It's early, Adler.

**RUTH (ON PHONE):** Victor Hale is dead.

Ward doesn't answer. Rain.

**RUTH (ON PHONE):** Sir?

**WARD:** Say it again.

**RUTH (ON PHONE):** Hale. In his room on Calder Street. Brennan wanted you to hear it before the papers.

**WARD:** Brennan wants me off it.

**RUTH (ON PHONE):** He didn't say that.

**WARD:** He will.

He hangs up. The faint, heavy thunk of a pin plays under the dial tone.

**GAMEPLAY:** First free exploration. The apartment is small; every object has a line.


- **The envelope on the desk.** Typed label: "STATE CRIME LABORATORY, COLD CASE UNIT. Det. Elias Ward." The postmark is readable: January 12. Interacting brings up a single prompt: _Not tonight._ It's morning. The prompt says it anyway.
- **The fridge.** A pencil sketch taped at eye level: Ward asleep on the building's front stairs, a newspaper over his chest. Signed "L.M." **WARD (V.O.):** Lily Marsh drew that the summer she was fifteen. Said I snored like a radiator.
- **The bathroom.** A sink, a towel. Above the sink, four screw holes and a pale rectangle where a mirror used to hang. Ward splashes water on his face. The camera holds on the blank wall one beat too long.
- **The flashlight** in his coat pocket. Long, black, heavy. A small dent near the lens. **WARD (V.O.):** Twenty years on the job. Dropped it on the stairs, probably.

Three soft knocks at the door. The same rhythm as the nightmare.

Ward opens it. The chain is already off.

WALT DOYLE, 60s, the building super: cardigan, tool belt, reading glasses on a cord, two paper cups of coffee. A kind, crinkled face. The knuckles of his right hand are bruised purple.

**WALT:** Heard a thump last night. Figured I'd make sure you were still breathing.

**WARD:** Fell asleep in the chair.

**WALT:** _(nods past him at the floor)_ The chair's over there, detective. _(hands him a coffee)_ Out late again?

**WARD:** I never left.

**WALT:** Sure. _(sips)_ You look like hell. Everything alright?

**WARD:** Victor Hale's dead.

Walt goes still. Just for a second. Then he sips his coffee.

**WALT:** Huh. _(beat)_ Guess some things finish themselves.

**WARD:** _(looking at Walt's knuckles)_ What happened to your hand?

**WALT:** Bar fight. Don't tell the landlord. _(winks)_ Eat something.

Walt walks off down the hall. As he passes the door of apartment 4C, he touches its frame lightly with two fingers, without looking at it, the way you'd touch something for luck.

Ward stands in his doorway looking at 4C. A faint rectangle of old glue marks where police tape once sealed it.

_Director's note: Walt must be the warmest person in the game. Players should like him more than anyone. Every line he says reads differently on a second playthrough._

## Chapter 1: Victor Hale

### 4. EXT. 19 CALDER STREET — DAY

A three-story rooming house with peeling green paint. Two patrol cars, lights turning without sirens. A small crowd under umbrellas.

On the ground-floor door, the ghost of old spray paint shows through a scrubbed patch: CHILD KILLER. Someone tried to wash it off more than once.

RUTH ADLER, early 30s, waits under the awning. Gray wool coat, notebook, hair pinned back, precise in everything. She sees Ward coming and doesn't look surprised.

**RUTH:** You shouldn't be here, sir.

**WARD:** Neither should he. _(nods at the door)_

**RUTH:** Brennan's orders. Conflict of interest.

**WARD:** Brennan's orders are to keep me out of the papers. Any reporters here?

**RUTH:** _(beat)_ Not yet.

**WARD:** Then I'm not here.

She holds the door for him.

### 5. INT. HALE'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS

The heat hits first. The room is an oven. The radiator clanks and hisses, its dial turned all the way up. Condensation runs down the inside of the windows. The air is thick enough to see.

VICTOR HALE, 50s, lies face down just inside the front door in a T-shirt and one slipper. His right arm is stretched toward the door. His fingernails are broken, and there are fresh scratches in the floorboards in front of them.

DR. AMES, the medical examiner, 60s, bow tie, kneels by the body with the calm of a man who has seen everything twice.

**AMES:** Morning, Elias. Didn't think they'd let you in.

**WARD:** They didn't.

**AMES:** Blunt force. Back of the skull first, then the temple. Something long, heavy, metal. A pipe, a tire iron.

_Director's note: Ames says "long, heavy, metal" while the player's flashlight is in frame. Never draw attention to it._

**WARD:** Time?

**AMES:** In this heat? Liver temp says around eleven last night. Give or take an hour.

**RUTH:** _(writing)_ Eleven.

**AMES:** And look at his wrist. _(lifts Hale's left hand an inch: a pale band of skin where a watch sat for years)_ He wore one every day. It's gone.

**RUTH:** _(writing)_ No watch.

**GAMEPLAY:** Crime scene investigation. Examinable objects:


- **The body.** The reaching arm, the broken nails, the scratches, the pale band where the watch was. **WARD (V.O.):** He was trying to get out. They always try to get out.
- **The door.** No forced entry. The chain is off, the bolt drawn back from the inside. **WARD (V.O.):** He let his killer in.
- **The radiator.** Dial at maximum. The metal is too hot to touch. **WARD (V.O.):** Somebody wanted it hot in here.
- **The windowsill.** Three potted plants, black and limp, leaves curled. The window is latched.
- **The desk.** A stack of letters in block capitals: I KNOW WHAT YOU DID TO MY GIRL. Each is signed T.M. Under them, a copy of a harassment complaint Hale filed against Tom Marsh.

Ruth crouches by the plants.

**RUTH:** Who lets their plants freeze in a sauna?

**WARD:** Hale wasn't much of a gardener.

**RUTH:** They didn't die of neglect, sir. That's frost damage.

**WARD:** _(beat)_ Write it down.

She does.

**RUTH:** _(holding up a letter)_ Tom Marsh. Six years of these.

**WARD:** Six years of grief.

**RUTH:** He spray-painted that door twice. Hale filed complaints.

**WARD:** Hale filed complaints about everything.

Ward kneels by the body. For a moment, the player controls where he looks. If the player looks at Hale's face, Ward's voice-over says nothing at all.

### 6. INT. PRECINCT, HOMICIDE SQUAD ROOM — DAY

Third floor of an old precinct house. Tall windows streaked with rain, radiators knocking, phones ringing. A cork board by the window, empty except for Hale's photo.

CAPTAIN BRENNAN, 60, heavy, gray, gravel-voiced, waves Ward into his glass office.

**BRENNAN:** Close the door.

Ward does.

**BRENNAN:** You know what the first question upstairs was this morning?

**WARD:** Where was Ward.

**BRENNAN:** Where was Ward. _(turns his monitor around)_

Grainy booking-desk footage. The timestamp reads 23:02. Ward, in his coat, signs in a staggering drunk.

**BRENNAN:** Eleven-oh-two. You book one Edward Kowalski, drunk and disorderly. You do the paperwork. _(scrubs forward; the timestamp reads 00:10)_ You leave at ten after twelve. Ames says Hale died at eleven. So you're clear.

**WARD:** I didn't know I needed to be.

**BRENNAN:** Everybody needs to be. Hale was scum, nobody's crying, and you were here. Work it. Quietly.

**WARD:** And the conflict?

**BRENNAN:** What conflict? You were right about him. The jury was wrong. That's not a conflict, that's a record. _(beat)_ Every case you ever closed is holding up a wall in this city, Elias. Don't give anyone a reason to look at the wall.

Out in the squad room, Ruth has pulled a dusty evidence box onto Ward's desk: MARSH, LILY — 2005.

**GAMEPLAY:** Examine the old case file.


- **Lily's school photo.** Fifteen, freckles, a green cardigan.
- **Ward's handwritten notes.** "HALE, VICTOR — prior record — lives 2 blocks away — no alibi — IT'S HIM." The last two words are underlined three times.
- **A single line near the bottom of a page.** "DOYLE, WALTER — building super. Interviewed. No further action." **WARD (V.O.):** Walt couldn't hurt a fly. I already had my man.

Ruth watches him read.

**RUTH:** Sir, did anything come for you from the state lab?

**WARD:** Why would it?

**RUTH:** _(beat)_ No reason.

She holds his look a moment too long. Then she looks away.

**RUTH:** You look like death, sir. Did you see someone about the blackouts?

**WARD:** Doc says exhaustion. Says I should sleep more.

**RUTH:** And?

**WARD:** I told him I'd love to.

**RUTH:** Tom Marsh isn't answering his phone. His neighbor says he goes to the cemetery every evening.

### 7. EXT. ST. BRIGID'S CEMETERY — DUSK

Rain. Bare trees. Rows of wet stones going gray in the last light.

TOM MARSH, 58, stands at a small headstone holding a closed umbrella he isn't using and a bottle he is. The stone reads: LILY ROSE MARSH, 1990–2005. OUR GIRL.

Ward's flashlight beam slides across the wet stones toward him.

**TOM:** _(without turning)_ They said it on the radio. Hale. _(beat)_ I hope it hurt.

**WARD:** Where were you last night, Tom?

**TOM:** Here. I'm always here.

He turns. His knuckles are scraped raw.

**WARD:** Your hands.

**TOM:** I hit a wall. Walls don't file complaints. _(an ugly laugh)_

**WARD:** You wrote him letters. Six years of them.

**TOM:** You promised me, Elias. In my kitchen. You held my hand and promised me you'd get him.

**WARD:** I did get him. The system let him go.

**TOM:** And now somebody didn't.

He looks back at the stone. The rain gets heavier.

**TOM:** She liked you, you know. Drew you. Said you were the only one in that building who ever said hello to her.

Ward says nothing.

**TOM:** Arrest me or don't. I'm going to stay a while.

Ward walks away. The camera stays on Tom and the stone.

_Director's note: As Ward leaves, far back between the trees, a small figure in a green cardigan stands for a single frame and is gone. This is the only time Lily appears outside a nightmare before the finale._

### 8. INT. PRECINCT, HOMICIDE — NIGHT

The squad room is empty. One desk lamp. Rain on the windows. The cork board, lit from below.

Hale's photo sits in the center. Two suspect cards hang beside it: TOM MARSH, and GUS PELL, Hale's landlord, who was owed four months' rent and was heard shouting at Hale last week.

**GAMEPLAY:** The Board. The player drags evidence under each suspect: the letters, the scraped knuckles, the missing alibi, the landlord's rent ledger, his key to Hale's room. Each card shows Motive, Opportunity, and Means as they fill in. Pell's card already carries a patrolman's note: he spent the night at a card game on Dock Street, nine until two, with eight witnesses. His Opportunity column won't take anything. If the player tries, Ward's voice-over answers.

**WARD (V.O.):** Eight men at a card table say otherwise.

_Director's note: Pell's alibi covers eleven o'clock, the time Ames gave for Hale's death. The same lie that clears Ward clears Pell and leaves Tom Marsh with nowhere to stand. Never point it out; a player who works out the real time of death will see it._

**WARD (V.O.):** Motive: six years of it. Opportunity: no alibi. Means: two good hands.

When Tom's card is complete, the prompt appears: PIN SUSPECT.

**GAMEPLAY:** The player pins Tom Marsh, the only complete case. (If the player walks away instead, see Alternate beat: refusing to pin.)

The pin goes in with a thunk far too loud for the room. The red string snaps taut. Somewhere in the building, the radiator knocks three times.

**WARD (V.O.):** I was sure. I'm always sure.

### 9. INT. THE CARLYLE — FOURTH-FLOOR HALLWAY / APARTMENT 4F — NIGHT

Ward climbs the Carlyle's stairs, the same stairs as Lily's sketch. The fourth-floor hallway is long and dim, with one bulb flickering at the far end.

He passes 4C. The glue rectangle from the old police tape. The peephole is dark. He doesn't slow down, but the camera does.

Inside 4F, he locks the door and slides the chain on. The camera lingers on the chain, fastened.

He pours a drink and sits in the armchair facing the door. The envelope sits on the desk at the edge of the lamplight.

**GAMEPLAY:** The player can move around the apartment. Interacting with the envelope brings up: _Not tonight._

Ward settles back in the chair. The radiator ticks.

The blackout begins. The room tilts slowly, a few degrees, like a ship. The radiator knocks three times. Sound drains out of the world. The door seems to recede down a long, dark hallway that wasn't there a moment ago. The camera drifts toward it.

CUT TO BLACK.

## Night 1

### 10. NIGHTMARE — HALE'S ROOM

Fade in on heat. Steam, a red glow with no source, the low roar of a radiator that sounds like a furnace.

It's Hale's room, remembered wrong. The ceiling is a foot too low. The room is stretched to three times its length, so the front door sits at the far end of a long, narrow space. The radiator glows orange like a stove element. The windows are fogged solid.

On the windowsill, the three plants are green and alive.

_Director's note: The plants were black this morning. Nobody in the game ever mentions it. A player who notices has understood that this nightmare is set before the window was opened._

On the wall above the bed hangs a clock: **3:40.** The second hand twitches forward and snaps back, forward and back, like it's stuck on a single second.

At the far end, the front door. A thin line of cold hallway light shows under it. LILY stands with her back pressed flat against the door, arms at her sides, green cardigan hanging off one shoulder. Her face is half in shadow. She's whispering, fast and soft, and we can't make out a word.

The UI fades in, small and white: **PROTECT HER**, and a full bar beneath it.

**SOUND:** The Room's lamp hum sits under everything, barely audible.

The bed at the near end of the room sags. Something sits up in it.

THE MONSTER rises: stooped, taller than a man even bent over, wrapped in something like wet gray bedsheets that cling where a body should be. It has no face, just a smooth, dented blankness. Its arms hang to its knees. It moves in jerks, like film with missing frames.

It turns its blank face toward the door. Toward Lily.

**GAMEPLAY:** The fight begins.


- The monster lunges toward the door in bursts. The player must get between it and Lily.
- When the flashlight beam hits it (LIGHT, hold), it throws both long hands over its blank face and staggers backward, exactly the way a person shields their eyes from a flashlight.
- It fights like something cornered, not something hunting. It hurls whatever is near: a lamp, a chair, a stack of books, a coffee cup. It never strikes first unless the player blocks its path.
- If it gets past the player and reaches the door, it doesn't touch Lily. Its long fingers scrabble at the door handle beside her hip. Lily presses herself flat against the door and squeezes her eyes shut. The PROTECT HER bar drains fast, and the screen pulses red at the edges. The player has to STRIKE it to drag it back into the room.

**SOUND:** Its roar is low and grinding, like a voice through mud. Under the distortion, rhythm without words: one word, again and again.

Phase two: at half health, the room lengthens. The door slides farther away down the stretching floor, and the monster drops to all fours and crawls toward it, fast, dragging itself by its fingertips. Its nails leave scratches in the floorboards. The player chases it down the lengthening room, striking, pulling it back, again and again.

_Director's note: On every successful hit, cut in one frame (1/24 of a second) of a real man's face: Victor Hale, eyes wide, squinting into a flashlight beam. Never hold it longer than one frame. Never repeat the same frame twice in a row._

The final strike. The monster collapses face down at Lily's feet. One long arm stretches past her ankles toward the door. The fingertips scrape the floorboards once, twice, and stop.

The PROTECT HER bar holds. A low swell of strings: victory music, warm, almost heroic.

_Director's note: This must feel like a win. The player should feel like they saved her. Everything that happens later depends on this feeling being real._

Lily opens her eyes. She looks down at the arm reaching past her feet. Then she looks up at the player.

Her whisper sharpens for a moment. Two words come through, barely: _"...Mr. Ward..."_

Then the line of light under the door goes out.

Total darkness. Total silence.

Three soft knocks.

CUT TO BLACK.

## Interlude 1

### 11. INT. THE ROOM — NIGHT

The lamp. The hum. The black glass and the silhouette behind it.

Ward leans down to his hands and rubs his face. Below the frame, a chain clinks. In the glass, far back in the dark, the silhouette's head dips at the same moment.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Tell us about the dream.

**WARD:** You want my dreams now?

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Every detail.

**WARD:** _(leans back; so does the silhouette)_ There's a girl at a door. Lily. And something in the room with us. Something that wants to get to her.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** What does it look like?

**WARD:** Like nothing. Like somebody took a man and rubbed out the face.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** What room is it?

**WARD:** Hale's. The room I saw that morning. Only hotter. _(beat)_ And the plants were alive.

A pause on the intercom. Very faintly, the scratch of a pen on paper.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** The plants were alive.

**WARD:** It's a dream. _(beat)_ I walk a scene all day, I dream it all night. Twenty years of that.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** What did you do?

**WARD:** What I always do. I stopped it before it got to her.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Was there a clock?

**WARD:** Three-forty. The hand doesn't move.

A longer pause. The pen scratches again.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** And where were you at three-forty that night, detective?

**WARD:** On my floor. Out cold.

The silhouette sits perfectly still. So does Ward.

_Director's note: This is his confession, and every detail in it is true. Keep the silhouette soft-focus in the background of every shot, so its posture matches his without the player ever seeing both clearly at once._

## Chapter 2: Tom Marsh

### 12. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — MORNING

Gray light. Rain. Ward on the floor again, closer to the door this time, arm outstretched toward it.

The chain hangs loose. He looked at it fastened last night. The player did too.

**GAMEPLAY:** Free exploration.


- **His shoes by the door.** Soaked through, soles caked with mud, laces still tied tight. **WARD (V.O.):** Must've tracked it in from the cemetery.
- **The flashlight.** A second dent beside the first. **WARD (V.O.):** Dropped it again.
- **The envelope.** _Not tonight._

Three soft knocks. Walt, with two coffees.

**WALT:** Out late again, detective? Heard your door around three.

**WARD:** I never left. I passed out in the chair.

**WALT:** The chair. _(smiles)_ Sure.

Walt's eyes drop to the muddy shoes by the door, then come back up to Ward's face. His smile doesn't change.

**WALT:** You ought to see somebody about those spells.

**WARD:** I did. He says I'm tired.

**WALT:** Well. _(hands him the coffee)_ He's not wrong.

The phone rings. Under the ring, faint: the thunk of the pin.

**RUTH (ON PHONE):** Sir. Tom Marsh is dead.

### 13. INT. TOM MARSH'S ROW HOUSE — DAY

A narrow brick row house across the river. Patrol cars. MRS. DELANEY, 70s, a neighbor in a housecoat and rain boots, stands on the sidewalk hugging herself.

**MRS. DELANEY:** I saw his fingers. In the mail slot. Sticking out through the flap. _(beat)_ I thought he was waving.

Inside, the front hall is barely wide enough for one person. TOM MARSH lies face down at the foot of the front door, one arm raised, his fingers pushed through the brass mail slot from the inside.

_Director's note: Frame the first shot from the street side, the way Mrs. Delaney saw it: four gray fingers poking through a brass flap in the rain. Hold it. Then cut inside._

DR. AMES is already there.

**AMES:** Same as Hale. Long, heavy, metal. Two blows.

**WARD:** Time?

**AMES:** Between two and five. Don't hold me to it; it's an icebox in here.

The living room: a whole wall covered in framed photos of Lily. School pictures, birthdays, a beach, a Halloween costume, a hundred versions of the same freckled face. In the middle of the wall, at eye level, an empty nail and a clean rectangle on the faded wallpaper where one frame used to hang.

**RUTH:** Something was taken. Right here.

**WARD:** Souvenir.

**RUTH:** From a grieving father's wall?

**WARD:** He keeps souvenirs.

**RUTH:** He?

**WARD:** It's always a he, Adler.

**GAMEPLAY:** Crime scene investigation.


- **The front door.** No forced entry. Chain off, bolt open. **WARD (V.O.):** He opened the door again.
- **The empty nail.** The pale rectangle is about eight by ten inches. **WARD (V.O.):** One photo. Out of a hundred.
- **Tom's answering machine.** One new message, left yesterday afternoon. NADINE HALE's voice, shaking with fury: "It's Nadine Hale. My brother is dead and I know it was you. You waited six years. If the police won't do anything, I will." _Click._
- **A half-empty bottle on the kitchen table**, and a single glass.

Ruth stands at the door, looking at the bolt.

**RUTH:** He opened it at three in the morning. _(beat)_ Who do you open the door for at three in the morning?

**WARD:** Someone you know.

**RUTH:** _(looking at him)_ Or someone with a badge.

**WARD:** Careful, Adler.

### 14. INT. PRECINCT, BRENNAN'S OFFICE — DAY

Brennan behind his desk, reading glasses on, the Marsh file open. He doesn't look up.

**BRENNAN:** You pinned Tom Marsh at six last night. He was dead by four.

**WARD:** Somebody knew.

**BRENNAN:** Who saw your board?

**WARD:** Half the floor. The cleaners. Anybody who walked past.

**BRENNAN:** Then half the floor's a suspect. _(takes off his glasses)_ Take it home. Nobody sees that board but you.

**WARD:** You think there's a leak.

**BRENNAN:** I think two men are dead and one of them was your suspect for ten hours. _(sits back heavily)_ Hale's sister has called this building nine times since yesterday. She says Tom Marsh killed her brother.

**WARD:** And now Tom Marsh is dead.

**BRENNAN:** And now Tom Marsh is dead.

### 15. INT. NADINE HALE'S APARTMENT, ABOVE THE LAUNDROMAT — EVENING

A narrow staircase up from a 24-hour laundromat. Through the floor, the rhythmic thump of big industrial dryers, steady as a heartbeat. The apartment is small and very clean. Steam from below fogs the windows.

Photos of VICTOR HALE as a boy line the shelf: a gap-toothed kid holding a fish. Nurse's scrubs hang over a kitchen chair, a hospital ID badge clipped to the pocket: N. HALE, RN.

NADINE HALE, 45, exhausted, arms folded, meets them at the door. Ruth stays a step behind Ward, watching both of them.

**NADINE:** You've got some nerve.

**WARD:** Tom Marsh is dead.

**NADINE:** I heard. _(beat)_ I'm not sorry.

**WARD:** You left a message on his machine.

**NADINE:** I left a lot of messages. I say a lot of things. Six years my brother had "child killer" painted on his door, because you needed him to be guilty.

**WARD:** Your brother was guilty.

**NADINE:** My brother was a sad, strange man who lived two blocks from the wrong building. _(beat)_ Where was I last night? Here. Alone. Listening to the dryers. Arrest me for that.

The dryers thump below. She steps closer to Ward and lowers her voice.

**NADINE:** You killed my brother, detective. Maybe not with your hands.

Ward doesn't answer. Below them, the dryers thump three times and stop. Silence.

### 15A. EXT. LAUNDROMAT — NIGHT — WARD'S CAR

Rain on the windshield. Ward behind the wheel, engine off. Ruth gets in, then stops with the door half open.

**RUTH:** Give me a minute.

She goes back up the stairs. Ward watches through the rain: in the lit window above the laundromat, Ruth talks to Nadine, earnest, one hand on Nadine's arm. Nadine shakes her head.

Ruth comes back and gets in, wet.

**WARD:** What was that?

**RUTH:** I asked if she felt safe.

**WARD:** Safe from what?

**RUTH:** _(beat)_ From whoever's doing this.

Ward starts the car.

**RUTH:** She's grieving, sir.

**WARD:** She's lying.

**RUTH:** About what?

**WARD:** I don't know yet. But I'm sure.

### 16. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — NIGHT

The cork board now hangs on Ward's living room wall, beside the window. Hale's and Tom's photos sit in a row along the top: the victims. Two suspect cards hang below: NADINE HALE, and RAY KOSTIC, the bookie Tom Marsh owed eleven thousand dollars.

Ward works the board out loud, pacing, a glass in his hand. He's always talked through his cases.

**WARD:** Nadine Hale. Motive: her brother, and the threat's on tape. Opportunity: her alibi is a row of dryers. Means: she's a nurse. She'd know where to hit. _(beat)_ Kostic. Motive: money. But a bookie doesn't kill the man who owes him. Dead men don't pay.

**GAMEPLAY:** The Board. Kostic's Motive column won't take anything, and Ward has just said why. PIN SUSPECT on Nadine Hale, the only complete case.

Thunk. The string snaps taut. The radiator knocks three times.

**GAMEPLAY:** Interacting with the envelope: _Not tonight._

Ward locks the door and slides the chain on. He sits in the armchair.

The blackout. This time the camera holds on the fastened chain in the foreground, sharp, while Ward goes soft-focus behind it. The room tilts. The radiator knocks three times. The door recedes down its impossible hallway.

CUT TO BLACK.

## Night 2

### 17. NIGHTMARE — TOM'S HOUSE

Cold this time. Breath hangs in the air. The red glow is dimmer, like embers.

Tom's narrow front hall has become a corridor that runs for what looks like a hundred feet. Both walls are covered floor to ceiling in framed photos of Lily, hundreds of them, frame against frame, the same freckled face at every age. The front door is at the far end, with its brass mail slot at hip height and the line of cold light beneath it.

Lily stands with her back to the door, the mail slot beside her hip. The brass flap trembles, as if wind is pushing it from outside. There is no wind.

The clock hangs among the photos: **3:12.** The second hand twitches and snaps back.

**GAMEPLAY:** Before the fight, the player can walk the corridor. One frame stands out at eye level: a photo of Lily at fifteen, sitting on the Carlyle's front stairs, laughing, in her green cardigan. It hangs from a nail in the center of the wall. Examining it brings up one line of text: _Lily. The Carlyle stairs._ The glass in the frame shows no reflection.

_Director's note: This is the frame that was missing from Tom's wall this morning. The player saw the empty nail. Now they see what hung there. Nobody in the game will point it out until the finale._

A sound behind the player: heavy, uneven footsteps, and the clink of a bottle rolling across a floor.

THE MONSTER is different tonight. Broad, heavy, shoulders like a doorframe, lurching as if drunk. The wet gray wrappings hang off it like an old coat. Its blank face is dented deeper on one side.

Its roar is lower and wetter than last night's. Under the distortion, the same one-word rhythm, faster, broken by something like sobbing.

**GAMEPLAY:** The fight.


- It's slow but strong. It doesn't dodge the flashlight beam; it turns its broad back to the light and keeps pushing toward the door.
- It pulls frames off the walls and throws them. They shatter around the player, and each broken frame leaves a blank space on the wall.
- New this fight: when it reaches the door, it drops to its knees and forces its fingers through the mail slot, as if trying to reach the street. Lily presses back against the door, eyes shut, and the PROTECT HER bar drains twice as fast as last night.

Midway through, the horror beat: every photo of Lily on both walls slowly turns on its nail, one after another, with a soft chorus of scraping, until every framed face looks toward the front door. Not at the monster, and not at the player. At the door.

_Director's note: Cut in the single-frame flashes on each hit, as in Night 1: Tom Marsh, eyes screwed shut against a flashlight beam, one hand raised._

The final strike. The monster falls against the door and slides down it. Its fingers stay pushed through the mail slot. They twitch once and go still.

The victory strings play again, softer this time, almost uncertain.

Lily looks down at the fingers in the slot. Then up at the player. Her whisper comes through in fragments: _"...it's... please..."_

The line of light under the door goes out.

Three soft knocks.

CUT TO BLACK.

## Chapter 3: Nadine Hale

### 18. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F / FOURTH-FLOOR HALLWAY — MORNING

Ward on the floor, arm toward the door. The chain hangs loose.

**GAMEPLAY:** Free exploration.


- **His right hand.** A fresh cut across two knuckles, already scabbing. **WARD (V.O.):** Caught it on something.
- **The kitchen trash.** On top, a brown paper evidence bag, torn open and empty. **WARD (V.O.):** Evidence bag. I bring the job home.
- **The flashlight.** A third dent. The lens is scratched now. **WARD (V.O.):** Needs replacing.
- **The envelope.** _Not tonight._

He steps out into the hallway. WALT kneels at the old radiator outside 4C with a wrench and a rag, reading glasses on the end of his nose.

**WALT:** Morning. _(taps the radiator three times with the wrench, listens)_ Air in the line.

Ward looks at the door of 4C.

**WARD:** Somebody moving in?

**WALT:** Next month. Six years it sat empty. Nobody wanted it. _(beat, still working)_ You think about her?

**WARD:** Every night.

**WALT:** Yeah. _(goes back to the radiator)_ Me too.

_Director's note: Play this as two lonely men sharing a grief. Nothing sinister in the performance. On a second playthrough, it will be the most frightening scene in the game._

Ward's phone buzzes in his pocket. Under the buzz, faint: the thunk of the pin.

**RUTH (ON PHONE):** Nadine Hale.

### 19. INT. NADINE HALE'S APARTMENT — DAY

The dryers thump below, steady. Steam curls up through the floorboards. Crime scene tape across the stairwell.

NADINE HALE lies against the inside of her front door in a bathrobe. Her fingers are still hooked around the deadbolt, as if she was trying to turn it when she died. Her scrubs hang over the kitchen chair where they hung last night. The ID badge clip on the pocket is empty.

**AMES:** Same weapon. Between two and four.

**RUTH:** He took her badge.

**WARD:** Souvenir.

MRS. OCAMPO, 70s, owner of the laundromat, stands at the top of the stairs with a basket of folded sheets she doesn't seem to know she's holding.

**MRS. OCAMPO:** I do the overnight loads. I saw a man on these stairs around three. Tall. Long coat.

Ruth's eyes move to Ward's long coat. Just for a second.

**WARD:** Half this city wears a long coat.

**MRS. OCAMPO:** _(nodding at Ruth)_ And the lady detective. She came back last night, late. After midnight. Talked to Nadine a long time.

Ward turns to Ruth.

**RUTH:** I asked if she felt safe.

**WARD:** You didn't tell me you came back.

**RUTH:** You didn't ask, sir.

_Director's note: Ruth heard Ward pin Nadine over her wire and came back to warn her. Nadine didn't believe her. Ruth plays it as guilt, and the player should read it as guilt._

**GAMEPLAY:** Crime scene investigation.


- **The deadbolt.** Her fingers on it. The chain is off, the door unlocked. **WARD (V.O.):** She let him in. Then she tried to let herself out.
- **The empty badge clip.** **WARD (V.O.):** Hale's watch. Marsh's photo. Now her badge. He's building a collection.
- **A shoebox of letters in her closet**, all from DENNIS PRYCE, all before last spring. The last one, in heavy pen: YOU'LL SEE ME AGAIN. Tucked beside them: a restraining order, dated April.
- **The window.** Fogged from the laundromat steam. Someone drew a small face in the fog with one finger, days ago, now half faded.

**RUTH:** Dennis Pryce. Her ex. She took out an order on him last spring.

**WARD:** Does he own a long coat?

Ruth doesn't answer. She's looking at the badge clip.

**RUTH:** Where were you last night, sir?

**WARD:** Home. Asleep. Like every night.

**RUTH:** Right.

### 20. INT. PRECINCT, HOMICIDE SQUAD ROOM — DAY

The morning paper is on every desk. The headline, huge: **THE NIGHT JUDGE.** Beneath it: THIRD "PERSON OF INTEREST" FOUND DEAD. DOES THE KILLER HAVE A SOURCE INSIDE?

Brennan throws a copy onto Ward's desk.

**BRENNAN:** "The Night Judge." They gave him a name, Elias. You know what happens when they give them a name. _(beat)_ Everyone you name ends up in a body bag.

**WARD:** Three for three.

**BRENNAN:** Your board was in your apartment. Who's been in your apartment?

**WARD:** Nobody. Walt, the super. Adler, once.

**BRENNAN:** Then find out who's listening. _(beat)_ And the next name you give me gets a uniform on their door. I'm not losing a fourth.

### 21. INT. THE MERIDIAN — SIXTH-FLOOR HALLWAY / PRYCE'S APARTMENT — AFTERNOON

The Meridian: a tired twelve-story building with flickering hall lights and the smell of wet carpet. MR. FENN, 60s, the landlord, mops the hallway, a huge ring of keys on his belt.

**FENN:** Six-D. Knock loud. He doesn't answer for anybody.

Ward knocks. Three times, soft, evenly spaced. He doesn't seem to notice the rhythm.

DENNIS PRYCE, 40, wiry, unshaven, opens the door on the chain.

**PRYCE:** No.

**WARD:** Nadine Hale is dead.

A long pause. The chain comes off.

The apartment is cramped: a mattress, a TV playing the news with the sound low, a long black leather coat on a hook by the door. A tall window opens onto an old iron fire escape, every rung glazed with ice.

**PRYCE:** I haven't seen Nadine in a year. The order said five hundred feet. I stayed five hundred feet.

**WARD:** You wrote her letters.

**PRYCE:** Before the order! _(points at the TV)_ You know what they're saying on the news? Everybody you look at ends up dead. Everybody. The killer's got somebody inside. It's in the papers, man.

**WARD:** Then stop giving me reasons to look at you.

**PRYCE:** Where was I last night? Here. Alone. Like always.

Ward looks out at the fire escape. Six stories down, the alley. The ice on the rungs catches the gray light.

**WARD:** Landlord ought to salt that.

**PRYCE:** Landlord ought to do a lot of things.

On the way out, Ward passes Fenn in the hallway. Fenn nods. Ward nods back.

### 22. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — NIGHT

Two quick knocks. Not the usual rhythm.

Ruth, rain on her coat, a box of files in her arms.

**RUTH:** The Pryce jacket. And the Hale autopsies.

**WARD:** Put them anywhere. Coffee?

He goes into the kitchen. The player controls Ward at the counter. From the living room: the mechanical click and whir of a camera shutter.

**GAMEPLAY:** If the player turns around, they see through the kitchen doorway: Ruth standing at the board, photographing it with a small camera. The flash goes off twice.

**WARD:** Adler?

**RUTH:** _(lowering the camera)_ Reference. For the file.

She looks at the desk. At the envelope.

**RUTH:** Did you ever open that letter from the lab?

**WARD:** What letter?

**RUTH:** The one on your desk, sir.

**WARD:** Not tonight, Adler.

She looks at him for a long moment. Then she leaves without the coffee.

**GAMEPLAY:** Ward searches the apartment for a leak. Objects the player can search: under the desk, the phone, the lamp, behind the board, the radiator. Each search comes up empty. Last, the smoke detector on the ceiling.

Ward twists the cover off. Inside, wedged behind the battery: a small black transmitter the size of a matchbox. A white sticker on its side: PROPERTY OF METRO P.D. — TECHNICAL SERVICES.

**WARD (V.O.):** Somebody was listening. Somebody with a badge.

He stares at it. Then he puts the cover back on.

**WARD (V.O.):** I left it where it was. If somebody wanted a name, I'd give them one. And we'd be waiting.

### 22A. EXT. CORNER OF HOLLIS AND NINTH — NIGHT

A payphone under a streetlight, rain hammering its metal hood. Ward feeds it a coin.

**WARD:** Put a uniform on Dennis Pryce's door tonight. Quietly. Nobody knows but you.

**BRENNAN (ON PHONE):** _(beat)_ Done.

### 22B. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — NIGHT — LATER

Ward stands directly under the smoke detector, facing the board. He speaks clearly, a little louder than he needs to.

**WARD:** Dennis Pryce. Motive: the order, and the letters. Opportunity: no alibi, and a tall man in a long coat on Nadine's stairs. Means: a long coat has deep pockets.

_Director's note: Ward's own flashlight lives in his coat pocket. Never point it out._

**GAMEPLAY:** PIN SUSPECT. Pryce is the only suspect on this board.

Thunk. The string snaps taut. The radiator knocks three times.

Ward locks the door. The chain goes on. He sits in the armchair, facing the door.

The blackout: the room tilts, the knocks, the door receding down its hallway.

CUT TO BLACK.

## Night 3

### 23. NIGHTMARE — ABOVE THE LAUNDROMAT

The floor is beating. Slow, heavy, steady, like a heart the size of the building. Every thump shakes the floorboards under the player's feet, and steam pushes up between them.

It's Nadine's apartment, remembered wrong. The walls sweat. White sheets hang from the ceiling everywhere, dozens of them, on lines strung wall to wall. They form a damp, shifting maze between the player and the front door.

The clock hangs on a clothesline, turning slowly on a clothespin: **3:26.**

Through gaps in the sheets, the door. The line of cold light under it. And Lily.

But tonight she isn't facing the room. She's turned around, her back to the player, her forehead almost touching the door. She raises one hand and knocks on it from the inside.

Three soft knocks. A pause. Three soft knocks. Over and over, all through the fight.

_Director's note: Nobody explains the knocking. The player should feel it's the most frightening thing she's done, and not know why._

Something moves behind the sheets. A shadow, thin and fast, crosses from one sheet to the next.

THE MONSTER tonight is narrow and quick, all angles, its wrappings like a wet robe. It doesn't lunge. It hides. It darts between the hanging sheets and freezes whenever the flashlight beam sweeps near it.

**GAMEPLAY:** The fight.


- The player hunts it through the sheets with the flashlight. Caught in the beam, it throws its arms over its blank face and bolts for another gap.
- It throws whatever it passes: a laundry basket, a pot, a hairdryer on its cord.
- It tries to slip around the maze to the door. When it reaches the door, it doesn't touch Lily. It reaches past her shoulder, and its long fingers work frantically at the deadbolt. The PROTECT HER bar drains fast.
- Each time the player drags it back, a sheet tears loose and falls, and the maze gets smaller and the room gets more exposed.

_Director's note: Single-frame flashes on each hit: Nadine Hale in a bathrobe, one arm raised against a flashlight beam, mouth open._

The final strike. It crumples against the door, one hand still hooked on the deadbolt.

The floor stops beating. Silence. Every sheet in the room hangs still.

The victory strings begin, and then cut out after two notes, as if the music lost its nerve.

The monster makes one last sound. Its roar is pitched down, as always, but only halfway this time. For one syllable, under the distortion, the player hears a woman's voice, almost clear: _"...please..."_

Lily stops knocking.

She turns around. She looks at the player for a long time. Her whisper, the clearest it has been: _"...Mr. Ward... please... it's..."_

The line of light goes out.

Three soft knocks.

CUT TO BLACK.

_Director's note: This is the first moment a player might truly suspect Ward. Let them. Chapter 4 will seem to confirm it, and Chapter 5 will seem to prove them wrong._

**WARD:** The papers were right.

## Chapter 4: The Arrest

### 25. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — MORNING

Ward on the floor, arm toward the door. The chain hangs loose.

**GAMEPLAY:** Free exploration.


- **His coat.** Soaked through, heavier than it should be. An orange-brown smear of rust across one cuff. **WARD (V.O.):** The fire escape at the Meridian. Everything in that building is rusted.
- **The flashlight.** No new dent. The first morning without one. Ward doesn't comment.
- **The envelope.** _Not tonight._

The phone rings. No thunk under it this time.

**BRENNAN (ON PHONE):** Pryce is alive. The uniform sat on his door all night. Nobody came.

Ward closes his eyes. Exhales for what feels like the first time in the game.

**BRENNAN (ON PHONE):** You hear me, Elias? Nobody came.

**WARD:** I hear you.

He hangs up. He looks up at the smoke detector on the ceiling.

**WARD (V.O.):** Nobody came. Because he knew we'd be waiting. He heard every word.

Ward drags a chair under the smoke detector, pries the transmitter loose, and drops it into an evidence bag. He seals it.

### 26. INT. PRECINCT, HOMICIDE SQUAD ROOM — DAY

The room is full: detectives on phones, uniforms at the coffee machine, a TV on a high shelf playing the morning news with the sound down.

Ward walks in holding the sealed evidence bag up where people can see it.

**WARD:** I found your leak.

The room slows. Heads turn.

Ruth steps into his path. Two uniformed officers stand a pace behind her. She looks exhausted, like she hasn't slept in days.

**RUTH:** Detective Ward. I need you to come with me.

**WARD:** Adler.

She looks at the bag in his hand.

**RUTH:** That's mine.

The squad room goes silent. A phone rings somewhere and nobody answers it.

**RUTH:** I put it in your smoke detector the day Hale died.

She holds up a small cassette recorder and presses play. On the tape: a room tone, a radiator ticking, then the rattle of a door chain sliding out of its track. A door opening. A door closing. Silence.

**RUTH:** January fifteenth, two-fifty-eight a.m. Your door. January sixteenth, three-oh-two. _(beat)_ Every night someone died, your door opened before three.

**WARD:** I never left.

**RUTH:** Last night. Two-fifty-one. _(she stops the tape)_ Where did you go, Elias?

Ward opens his mouth. Nothing comes. For the first time in the game, he has no answer.

BRENNAN comes out of his office.

**BRENNAN:** Adler, what the hell is this?

**RUTH:** It's enough to hold him.

Ward looks at Brennan. Brennan looks at the tape recorder, then at the floor.

**BRENNAN:** _(quietly)_ Hold him.

The uniforms step forward. The camera stays tight on Ward's face. Off-frame, the metallic ratchet of handcuffs closing: once, twice.

_Director's note: Keep the cuffs off-screen, sound only. The player will hear this exact sound again in the final nightmare, and see it for the first time in the finale._

As they walk him out, he passes under the TV. A REPORTER stands outside the precinct in the rain, and the closed captions read: "...SOURCES CONFIRM A HOMICIDE DETECTIVE HAS BEEN DETAINED IN CONNECTION WITH THE SO-CALLED NIGHT JUDGE KILLINGS..."

Ward sees it. The camera holds on the screen after he's gone.

### 27. INT. HOLDING CELL — NIGHT

Concrete. A steel bench. A caged bulb in the corridor outside the bars. Somewhere, a drip. A clock on the corridor wall, in a wire cage.

Ward lies on the bench on his back, coat folded under his head. His eyes close.

He doesn't black out. He sleeps.

**GAMEPLAY:** The player has no control. The camera holds on Ward from the corridor, through the bars, and doesn't move.

The corridor clock: 11:40. Then 12:55. Then 1:30. The drip. The hum of the caged bulb. Nothing else.

No nightmare. No monster. No Lily. No knocks.

At 2:04 by the corridor clock, very far away, a siren rises and fades. Ward doesn't stir.

_Director's note: This is the quietest scene in the game. No music at all. Hold it for at least ninety seconds. Players are waiting for the nightmare, and its absence should feel worse than any monster. Let them wonder if the game has frozen._

### 28. INT. HOLDING CELL — DAWN

Pale light at the end of the corridor. A GUARD drags a baton across the bars. The clatter is deafening after the silence.

**GUARD:** Ward. Up.

Ward sits up. He looks rested for the first time in the game. His face is almost young.

**GUARD:** Your guy Pryce is dead.

**WARD:** _(beat)_ What?

**GUARD:** Went out his window around two. Fire escape. Captain wants you upstairs.

The guard unlocks the cell. Ward doesn't move for a moment. He's looking at his own hands.

**WARD (V.O.):** I was in a cage. I was asleep in a cage with a guard outside. And he still got to him.

The thunk of the pin, very faint, under the sound of the cell door rolling open.

## Chapter 5: The Fall

### 29. INT. PRECINCT, BRENNAN'S OFFICE — MORNING

Brennan behind his desk, gray-faced, a coroner's preliminary report in front of him. Ward stands by the window, uncuffed, rubbing one wrist. Ruth stands in the middle of the room like someone waiting for a verdict.

**BRENNAN:** Dennis Pryce went down an iced-over fire escape at two in the morning with a uniform sitting outside his door. Neighbors heard screaming. His place was torn apart. _(to Ruth)_ And the man you say is the Night Judge was asleep in my holding cell.

**RUTH:** Sir, the tapes—

**BRENNAN:** An illegal wire in a detective's home. Recordings of a door. That's your case? _(beat)_ That's not a case, Adler. That's a grudge.

**RUTH:** It's not a grudge.

**BRENNAN:** Badge and gun. You're suspended.

She unclips her badge and sets it on the desk. Then her gun beside it. She doesn't take her eyes off Ward.

At the door, she stops.

**RUTH:** Open the letter, Elias.

She leaves. The glass door swings shut behind her.

**BRENNAN:** _(rubbing his eyes)_ Go find me the son of a bitch who did this. Before the papers find out I locked up the wrong man.

### 30. EXT. / INT. THE MERIDIAN — DAY

**The alley.** Gray morning. Pryce's body lies under a yellow tarp on the frozen asphalt. Above, six stories of iron fire escape glitter with ice. At the sixth floor, a window stands wide open, a curtain blowing out of it into the cold.

OFFICER BELL, 20s, the uniform who guarded Pryce's door, stands by the tarp, pale.

**BELL:** I never left the door, sir. I swear to God. I heard him moving around in there all night. Drawers. Footsteps. _(beat)_ Around two I knocked, just to check on him. Three times, soft. Didn't want to wake the floor. _(beat)_ Then screaming. Then nothing.

_Director's note: Bell doesn't know the rhythm means anything. Pryce had watched the news all day: the killer is a cop, and at two in the morning a cop was knocking on his door._

**WARD:** Did you go in?

**BELL:** Door was bolted from the inside. By the time the super opened it— _(he looks up at the window)_

**The apartment.** Wrecked. Drawers pulled out and dumped. Clothes everywhere. A suitcase open on the mattress, half full. The TV still on, replaying the morning news: the rain, the precinct, DETECTIVE DETAINED. The hook by the door is empty; the long leather coat is gone.

**GAMEPLAY:** Investigation.


- **The suitcase.** Half-packed, clothes thrown in without folding. **WARD (V.O.):** Maybe he was packing. Or maybe the Judge wanted it to look that way.
- **The window.** Wide open. On the first iron landing outside, one of Pryce's shoes, frozen to the grate. **WARD (V.O.):** The Judge came in through the window. Pryce ran. The ice did the rest.
- **The TV.** The detained-detective story, on a loop. Ward turns it off.
- **The door.** Bolted from the inside when Bell found it. **WARD (V.O.):** Nobody came through that door. So he came through the window.

MR. FENN, the landlord, appears in the doorway, his keys jingling.

**FENN:** Terrible. Terrible thing. _(beat)_ You know, a fella came by the night before last. Late. Flashed a badge. Said he was from the city, checking the boiler. I let him down in the basement.

**WARD:** What did he look like?

**FENN:** Tall. Long coat. _(shrugs)_ I looked at the badge, not the face.

**WARD:** Show me the basement.

**The basement.** Fenn leads him down: a huge, low room, a boiler the size of a truck breathing and ticking, a maze of pipes along the ceiling. At the far end, a narrow steel door to the back stairwell, the one that climbs all twelve floors.

**WARD (V.O.):** He wasn't checking the boiler. He was looking for another way up.

**WARD (V.O.):** A badge. The papers were right. The Night Judge was a cop.

### 31. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — NIGHT

Ward at the board. He moves Pryce's photo up into the row of victims: Hale, Marsh, Hale, Pryce. The space below, where suspects go, is empty.

**GAMEPLAY:** The PIN SUSPECT prompt appears, grayed out. If the player tries to use it, Ward's voice-over answers.

**WARD (V.O.):** Not until I know who's listening.

_Director's note: The grayed-out prompt applies to suspects. A complete case against Ward's own ID can still be pinned here (see Secret ending)._

**GAMEPLAY:** Interacting with the envelope: _Not tonight._

For the first time in the game, Ward doesn't sit in the armchair. He walks into the bedroom, a room the player has never seen: a narrow bed, a crucifix on the wall, unopened mail stacked on the dresser. He lies down on top of the covers in his clothes.

The camera watches from the bedroom doorway. His breathing slows. He sleeps.

No blackout. The room doesn't tilt. The picture simply fades, the way sleep does.

## Night 5

### 32. NIGHTMARE — THE BASEMENT

The Meridian's boiler room, remembered. It's bigger than it was this afternoon, and darker. A single bare bulb swings slowly on its cord, though there's no draft. The boiler breathes: a long, slow inhale, a hiss, a tick.

Overhead, the pipes knock. Three soft knocks. A pause. Three soft knocks, echoing down the length of the room.

The clock hangs on a pipe: **3:05.**

At the far end, the steel door to the back stairwell. The line of cold light under it. Lily stands in front of it, facing the player.

There is no monster. There is no PROTECT HER bar. No UI of any kind.

**GAMEPLAY:** The player can walk anywhere. They can examine the boiler, the pipes, the stairwell door (it's locked). They can shine the flashlight into every dark corner. Nothing is there.

From far above, impossibly clear through six stories of concrete, come slow footsteps: someone pacing a hallway. The crackle of a police radio. A young man's voice, bored: "Six-D, all quiet."

Lily doesn't whisper tonight. She just watches the player, wherever they go. When they come close, she doesn't move aside.

After about two minutes, the bulb goes out.

_Director's note: This is the most frightening nightmare in the game, and nothing happens in it. Players have been trained to expect a monster and a fight. Let them wait for one that never comes. The empty room is the clue: this is the only crime scene in the game where Ward did nothing._

### 33. INT. THE CARLYLE, WARD'S BEDROOM — 3:00 A.M.

Ward's eyes open. He's in his bed, not on the floor. The clock on the nightstand reads 3:00.

He gets up and walks to the living room. The chain is on the door. His shoes are dry.

He goes to the window. Rain. Across the street, under the streetlight, a figure stands with no umbrella, rain running off her gray wool coat: RUTH. She's looking up at his window.

They look at each other through the rain and the glass.

She doesn't move. She doesn't wave. She doesn't leave.

Ward pulls the blind down.

Behind him, the radiator knocks three times.

### 34. INT. THE ROOM — NIGHT

**WARD:** In a cell. Ask your people.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** We did.

**WARD:** Then you know I couldn't have—

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** We know.

## Chapter 6: The Last Pin

### 35. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — MORNING

Ward wakes in his bed. Pale winter sun, the first sun in the game, lies in a stripe across the floor.

**GAMEPLAY:** Free exploration. Everything that has been wrong every morning is right.


- **The chain.** Fastened.
- **His shoes.** Dry, side by side, laces loose.
- **His coat.** On its hook, dry.
- **The flashlight.** No new dent.
- **The kitchen trash.** Nothing in it but coffee grounds.
- **The envelope.** _Not tonight._

_Director's note: This is the only morning in the game where nothing is wrong. Players have learned to hunt for the wrong detail. Let them search and find nothing. It should be more unsettling than any of the others._

Three soft knocks. Walt, with two coffees.

**WALT:** You slept.

**WARD:** Nobody died.

Walt looks at him for a long moment.

**WALT:** That's a first too. _(hands him the coffee)_ Saw your partner out there last night. In the rain. Standing like a statue.

**WARD:** I saw her.

**WALT:** _(glancing past Ward at the board)_ Folks do strange things this time of year. _(beat)_ You be careful, detective.

He goes. Ward closes the door and turns to the board.

He pins up a new suspect card: RUTH ADLER. Beside it, two more: MR. FENN, the landlord who let a stranger into his basement, and OFFICER BELL, the only man who was at Pryce's door all night.

**GAMEPLAY:** The Board. The player arranges evidence. Under Ruth's card, the evidence fills in fast:


- A police equipment log: transmitter, serial ending 0417, signed out to DET. R. ADLER.
- Her tapes.
- "Went back to Nadine Hale's apartment alone the night she died."
- "Photographed the board."
- "Outside my building, 3 a.m."
- "Asked about the lab letter before I knew it existed."

**WARD:** _(out loud, pacing)_ Means: she carries a badge, and she's been in my apartment. Opportunity: she was at Nadine's the night she died. Motive… _(beat)_ I'll find the motive.

He takes a blank index card, writes a single question mark on it, and pins it under MOTIVE. The board accepts it. Fenn's and Bell's cards each hold a line or two, but neither has a motive, and Ward doesn't write them one.

_Director's note: This is the only evidence card in the game that Ward writes himself. It's the moment his certainty stops needing a case._

**GAMEPLAY:** PIN SUSPECT on Ruth Adler, the only complete case.

The pin goes in. It's the loudest thunk in the game.

The radiator doesn't knock. Silence.

### 36. INT. PRECINCT, BRENNAN'S OFFICE — DAY

**WARD:** It's Adler.

Brennan stares at him for a long time.

**BRENNAN:** Jesus, Elias.

**WARD:** She planted the wire. She was at Nadine's. She was outside my building at three in the morning. She knew about that letter before I did.

**BRENNAN:** What letter?

**WARD:** _(beat)_ It doesn't matter.

**BRENNAN:** _(rubs his face with both hands)_ Everyone you name ends up dead. So she gets a detail. Tonight. _(beat)_ God help me.

**BRENNAN:** And if you're wrong—

**WARD:** I'm not wrong. I'm never wrong.

### 37. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — NIGHT

Ward locks the door. Slides the chain on. The camera holds on it: fastened.

**GAMEPLAY:** Interacting with the envelope: _Not tonight._

He sits in the armchair facing the door. The lamp.

Out in the hallway, footsteps. Under the door, the line of light from the hall. A shadow stops in it: someone standing just outside. It stays there. Five seconds. Ten. Then it moves on, and the footsteps go down the hall toward 4C.

## Night 6

### 38. NIGHTMARE — RUTH'S APARTMENT

A place the player has never seen. A small, neat apartment: bookshelves, a reading chair, a kettle, plants on the sill, green and alive. It's the first nightmare set somewhere the player never investigated.

On one wall hangs a cork board. Ruth's own board. At its center: a photo of Elias Ward. Red strings run from his face to every victim: Hale, Marsh, Nadine, Pryce.

_Director's note: Players will read this as proof that Ruth was obsessed with framing Ward. That's exactly what they should think._

The clock on the kitchen wall: **3:03.**

Lily stands in front of the door. The line of cold light beneath it. She isn't whispering. She's looking at the player, and her face is completely calm.

THE MONSTER stands up from the reading chair. It's smaller than the others. Almost human-sized. It stands upright, balanced, its hands raised in front of its blank face like a boxer.

It doesn't run for the door. It holds its ground between the player and the rest of the room.

**GAMEPLAY:** The fight.


- The monster fights back directly, for the first time. It blocks the flashlight beam with a raised forearm instead of cowering. It strikes the player, hard and fast, and the player's health drops for real.
- It never retreats toward the door. It stays between the player and Lily, as if it's the one guarding something.
- There is no retry. When the player's health reaches zero, the screen cracks like glass, and the fight continues.

Halfway through, behind Lily, the deadbolt turns on its own. From the outside.

The door bursts open.

SHADOW FIGURES pour through: tall, many, faceless, each one carrying its own flashlight. A dozen beams cross the room and hit the player all at once. The screen whites out at the edges. For the first time, the player is the one shielding their eyes.

Lily looks at the player one last time. Then she dissolves into the light pouring in from the hall, like smoke in a draft.

**SOUND:** Voices through the distortion, pitched down and overlapping: "DOWN! GET DOWN!" "HANDS!" "WARD, DON'T MOVE!"

The camera drops to the floor. The player's view is level with the floorboards, looking at the open door and the light beyond it. It is the exact angle of every body in the game.

White.

In the white, one sound: the metallic ratchet of handcuffs closing. Once. Twice.

CUT TO:

### 38A. INT. THE ROOM — NIGHT

The lamp. The hum. Ward, calm, almost satisfied.

**WARD:** We finally got him. _(beat)_ At Ruth's place.

## Finale

### 39. INT. THE ROOM — NIGHT

The lamp. The hum. The silhouette.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** One last question, detective.

**WARD:** Ask it.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Who killed Victor Hale?

Ward raises his hands as far as they'll go and points at the silhouette with both of them. Below the frame, a chain snaps taut with a small metal clank. The camera stays on his face; his hands are out of frame. In the glass behind him, the silhouette's hands rise too, pointing back.

**WARD:** He did.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Then turn off your lamp and look at him.

Ward doesn't move.

**GAMEPLAY:** A single prompt: TURN OFF THE LAMP. Nothing else is possible. If the player waits ten seconds, the voice speaks again, softer.

**VOICE (INTERCOM):** Elias.

The player presses the button.

Click. The lamp dies. Ward's side of the glass goes black. The hum stops. For the first time in the game, the Room is completely silent.

A second click, from the other side.

Warm light rises behind the glass: soft, yellow, ordinary. It's the first warm light in the entire game.

The silhouette is gone.

Behind the glass is a small observation room. RUTH sits at a microphone, her left arm in a sling, a dark bruise along her cheekbone. BRENNAN stands behind her, gray, holding his hat in both hands. DR. REYES, 50s, calm, a notepad on her knee, sits beside a cassette recorder whose reels are still turning.

All three are looking through the glass at Ward. The way he looked at the silhouette.

Then the camera does what it has never done. It tilts down.

Ward's hands lie on the steel table, cuffed. The chain between the cuffs runs through a steel ring bolted to the tabletop. His shirt has no tie. Under the table, his shoes have no laces.

Ward looks up at the glass. It's just glass now.

Ruth leans toward the microphone. For the first time, her voice comes through clear, with no distortion.

**RUTH:** You just did.

**WARD:** _(barely)_ What?

**RUTH:** The first thing we asked you. Tell us about the killer. _(beat)_ You just did.

**RUTH:** You described every murder, Elias. You just called them dreams.

**WARD:** No. _(beat)_ No. I was on my floor.

**DR. REYES:** _(leaning to the microphone, gently)_ Elias, my name is Dr. Reyes. You've been in this room since four o'clock this morning. You're safe. Nobody here is going to hurt you. _(beat)_ We let you tell it your way, so you could hear it.

Ward looks down at the cuffs.

**WARD:** Four this morning. _(beat)_ At Ruth's place.

**RUTH:** At my place. _(beat)_ The detail was waiting across the hall. You knocked three times, soft, and I opened the door. _(beat)_ Like all of them.

Behind Ward, the steel door with no handle buzzes and unlocks.

Ruth comes in with a cardboard box under her good arm. She sits down across the table from him. She isn't afraid of him. She's just very tired.

She takes a framed photo out of the box and sets it in front of him: Lily at fifteen on the Carlyle's front stairs, laughing, in her green cardigan. The glass is cracked across one corner.

**RUTH:** This hung on Tom Marsh's wall. We found it in your closet this morning.

She takes out more. Brown paper evidence bags, one at a time. Each lands on the steel table with a soft thunk, the same sound as the pin. Each label is written in the same neat hand:


- HALE, V. — WRISTWATCH
- MARSH, T. — PHOTOGRAPH
- HALE, N. — HOSPITAL ID

Ward reads the labels.

**WARD:** _(barely)_ That's my handwriting.

**RUTH:** You bagged them like evidence. You were building cases, Elias. After.

Then she reaches into the box one last time and takes out the envelope. The one from his desk. Still sealed. She lays it in front of his cuffed hands.

**GAMEPLAY:** A single prompt: OPEN. For the first time in the game.

The player opens it. The letter fills the screen, typed on state letterhead:

> STATE CRIME LABORATORY — COLD CASE UNIT RE: MARSH, LILY R. (2005) Re-examination of retained biological evidence using current methods. RESULT: VICTOR HALE is EXCLUDED as the source of the male DNA profile. The profile was searched against the state offender database. MATCH: DOYLE, WALTER JAMES. Profile entered 12/09/2010 following an arrest for assault.

In the corner, the postmark: JAN 12.

**RUTH:** I asked for that retest. I wanted to prove you right. _(beat)_ It came the day before Hale died. You never opened it. _(beat)_ But part of you knew.

Ward stares at the name.

**WARD:** Walt. _(beat)_ Walt brings me coffee.

**RUTH:** I know.

**DR. REYES:** _(over the speaker)_ Elias. Do you remember the night Lily died?

**WARD:** I was asleep.

**DR. REYES:** Were you?

Ward closes his eyes. Very faintly, the hum returns.

CUT TO:

### 40. INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — SIX YEARS EARLIER — NIGHT

The same apartment, six years younger. Fewer bottles. A working lamp. A ballgame murmuring on a small TV. On the fridge, Lily's sketch, the paper still white.

WARD, six years younger, darker hair, sits in the armchair facing the door with a glass in his hand. He's drunk. Not falling-down drunk. Just gone.

Three soft knocks at the door. The same rhythm the player has heard all game.

Ward's eyes open. He looks at the door. He doesn't get up.

**WARD:** _(slurred, not unkind)_ Not tonight, kid.

A pause. Then through the wood, LILY's voice: small, frightened, fifteen.

**LILY (O.S.):** Please, Mr. Ward. _(beat)_ It's Walt. _(beat)_ I'm scared of him.

Ward's eyes close. The glass tilts in his hand.

Under the door, the thin line of hallway light, the same line from every nightmare. A small shadow stands in it for a long moment.

Then the shadow moves away. Her footsteps go back down the hall. Slow. Then gone.

CUT TO BLACK on the sound of the last footstep.

_Director's note: No music. Hold five full seconds of silence before the credits. The last thing the player sees Ward do is nothing. Lily's line here, played in reverse, is the whisper from every nightmare in the game._

## Credits

### 41. THE CASE BOARD / THE REPLAYS

Darkness. Then the case board, lit by a single low light, exactly as it hung on Ward's wall. The credits begin to roll up the right side of the screen, small and white.

One by one, every red string the player tied comes loose with a soft twang. Each one drifts across the board and re-ties itself to a single photo pinned at the center: Ward's booking photo. No tie. Red eyes. He's looking straight into the camera.

_Director's note: Tie the strings to the player's own playthrough: every pin they placed, plus any pin Ward placed himself when they refused._

Then the board fades, and the replays begin.

No filter. No red glow. No music. Grainy, handheld, dim, the texture of real footage. Each replay opens on a front door, seen from the hallway side. Lily is never in any of them. Each replay is staged from the player's own recorded movement in that nightmare, mapped out of the stretched dream room into the real one.

**NADINE.** A narrow stairwell above a laundromat. The dryers thumping below. Three soft knocks. The door opens on its chain: Nadine in a bathrobe, squinting.

**NADINE:** Detective? It's three in the morning.

**WARD (O.S.):** I know. It's about Tom Marsh. Can I come in?

She hesitates. Then she slides the chain off.

Inside: a flashlight beam hits her face. She throws her arm up against the light and backs away. Sheets on a drying rack tip over. She runs for the door. A dark coat steps between her and it.

Cut to black before anything lands. The dryers keep thumping. On the kitchen wall, in the dark, a clock: 3:26.

**TOM.** A narrow row house, a brass mail slot. Three soft knocks. Tom opens the door, bleary, a bottle in his hand.

**TOM:** Elias? What are you—

The beam in his eyes. The bottle rolls across the floorboards. The hallway is too narrow for two men; the struggle is all elbows and walls. Tom crawls for the front door. His fingers push through the mail slot toward the street. A long shadow falls across his hand.

Cut to black. A clock: 3:12.

**HALE.** The last replay is framed shot for shot like the cold open. The heat. The radiator. Three plants on the windowsill, green and alive. Three soft knocks. Hale opens the door in a T-shirt and one slipper. He isn't surprised.

**HALE:** _(flat, quiet)_ Detective Ward. _(beat)_ I wondered when.

The beam. Hale throws a lamp; it shatters against the wall. He runs for the door. Ward is already between him and it.

Cut to black. In the dark, a window latch turns. Cold air moves the curtain, and the three green plants on the sill shiver. The clock: **3:40.** The second hand ticks, but never moves forward.

Hold on the clock.

Then the title, white on black: **CONVICTION.**

_Director's note on violence: Never show a blow land. Cut on the moment before, every time, and let the sound design and the clock carry it. What should horrify the player is how ordinary it looks: a tired man at a door at three in the morning, and someone who opens it because he has a badge. They'll recognize this last replay: they played it in the first five minutes of the game._

## Secret ending

### 42. THE ONLY CASE YOU CAN SOLVE

**Trigger:** On any night, from Chapter 1 onward, the player takes Ward's police ID out of the inventory and puts its photo on the board as a suspect. Like any other case, it can only be pinned once its Motive, Opportunity and Means are filled, from what the mornings and the old case file leave behind: "IT'S HIM" underlined three times in Ward's notes (motive), the chain hanging loose and Walt hearing the door at three (opportunity), a flashlight that is long, heavy, metal and newly dented (means). This works in Chapter 5 too, where every other pin is refused. Nothing in the game suggests any of this is possible. The board simply accepts it.

**INT. THE CARLYLE, APARTMENT 4F — NIGHT**

The pin goes in. The thunk is quiet this time, almost soft. The string doesn't snap taut; it just hangs.

The radiator doesn't knock.

Ward stands in front of the board, looking at his own face in the middle of it. He stands there a long time.

**WARD (V.O.):** For the first time in my life, I wasn't sure.

He doesn't sit in the armchair. He picks up the envelope from the desk, and for the first time, it doesn't say _Not tonight._ It just goes into his coat pocket. He puts on the coat and opens the door.

**INT. THE CARLYLE — FOURTH-FLOOR HALLWAY — CONTINUOUS**

WALT is in the hallway outside 4C, in his cardigan, a flashlight of his own in his hand, making his late rounds. He looks up.

**WALT:** Out late again, detective?

Ward looks at him for a long moment.

**WARD:** Yes.

He walks past Walt and down the stairs. Walt watches him go. For the first time, Walt doesn't smile.

**EXT. CITY STREETS — 3:00 A.M.**

Rain. Empty streets. Traffic lights changing for no one. The camera follows Ward from behind, a long, unbroken walk: no music, only rain, his footsteps, and the envelope's corner showing at his pocket.

**GAMEPLAY:** The player walks him there. The route is simple. The game doesn't hurry them.

**INT. PRECINCT — FRONT DESK — NIGHT**

Fluorescent lights. A DESK SERGEANT, 40s, halfway through a crossword, looks up.

**DESK SERGEANT:** Detective? It's three in the morning.

Ward sets three things on the counter, one at a time: his badge. His gun. His flashlight, dented near the lens.

**WARD:** I need to report some murders.

**DESK SERGEANT:** _(slowly putting down his pen)_ Whose?

**WARD:** I don't know all of them yet. Start with Victor Hale.

He takes the envelope out of his pocket. He opens it himself, standing at the desk. He reads it. A long beat. His face doesn't change, except around the eyes.

**WARD:** And there's one more. Six years ago. Lily Marsh.

He slides the letter across the counter.

**WARD:** You'll want to pick up a man named Walter Doyle. He's the super in my building. _(beat)_ Then take me downstairs.

**EPILOGUE — MONTAGE — MORNING**

The first real daylight in the game. Soft, warm, ordinary. No music but a single held piano note.

Everyone who would have died after the night Ward pinned himself is alive, and the montage shows only them:


- Tom Marsh at St. Brigid's, laying fresh flowers on Lily's stone, sober, in the sun.
- Nadine Hale in scrubs, locking her door on her way to a shift. The dryers hum below.
- Dennis Pryce on a bus with a suitcase on his knees, watching the city go by.
- Ruth, her badge back on her belt, walking down the holding-cell corridor with two coffees. She stops at Ward's cell.

In the Carlyle's hallway, two uniforms lead WALT out past 4C in handcuffs. At the stairwell, he looks back at 4C one last time.

**INT. HOLDING CELL — MORNING**

Ward asleep on the bench, coat under his head, sunlight on the concrete. Ruth sets one coffee on the floor inside the bars, quietly, so she won't wake him.

No nightmare. No knocks. The picture fades slowly to warm white.

**CONVICTION.**

_Director's note: This ending must feel small and quiet. No triumph. The man who was never wrong finally suspects himself, and it's the only thing in the game that saves anyone._
