# CONVICTION

Sep 27, 2026 · @Ziv

_Everyone says Detective Elias Ward has never been wrong about a suspect. He believes it more than anyone._

## Cast


- Elias Ward: a homicide detective whose record props up hundreds of the city's convictions. He lives alone, drinks too much, and keeps a drawing Lily made him taped to his fridge.
- Ruth Adler: his younger partner, who joined homicide because of him.
- Captain Brennan: Ward's boss.
- Lily Marsh: a fifteen-year-old who lived down the hall from Ward, murdered six years ago.
- Victor Hale: acquitted of Lily's murder on a technicality.
- Tom Marsh: Lily's father.
- Walt Doyle: the super in Ward's building, the closest thing Ward has to a friend.
- Dr. Reyes: a psychiatrist who appears in one scene.

## The frame

The game opens in an interrogation room. Ward sits at a desk with a lamp at his back, facing a one-way glass. Behind it, in the dark, sits a silhouette: the serial killer the papers call the Night Judge, finally in custody.

A crackly voice comes over the intercom: "Tell us about the killer, detective."

Everything you play is Ward's account, told in flashback, and the game cuts back to this room between chapters. The voice asks questions, Ward answers, and the silhouette never speaks. In these scenes, the camera never shows Ward's hands.

## Act 1: The Board

It starts with a nightmare: darkness, a teenage girl, a faceless monster, a clock reading 3:40. It plays like every horror game's cold open. Ward wakes on his apartment floor to a ringing phone: Victor Hale is dead.

Six years ago, Ward was certain Hale murdered Lily. Hale walked, and Ward never forgave the system. Now everyone looks at Ward.

Then the coroner puts Hale's time of death at 11 p.m., when Ward was on camera at the precinct booking a drunk. On Ward's desk sits a sealed envelope from the state crime lab. Every time you try to open it, the prompt says "Not tonight."

From here, every chapter runs the same loop:


1. You work the crime scene and interview witnesses.
2. You pin your prime suspect to the case board with red string.
3. Ward goes home and blacks out, which his doctor calls exhaustion.
4. By morning, the suspect you pinned is dead.

The night after you work a scene, you dream about it. Each nightmare is set inside the scene you just investigated, and Lily is always there. Your objective is to protect her from a faceless monster.

It reads as trauma: he couldn't save her, so every night he tries again. She always stands in front of the apartment's front door, whispering something you can't make out. The monster keeps lunging toward her while you block it.

Mornings belong to Walt, who knocks with coffee after Ward's bad nights. "Out late again, detective?" Ward says he never left; he passed out, and Walt just smiles.

Something small is always wrong in the apartment: a damp coat, mud on his shoes, the chain off the door.

Your first suspect is the obvious one: Tom Marsh, Lily's father, who spent six years threatening Hale. You pin him. Ward blacks out, the nightmare takes him back to Hale's apartment, and in the morning Tom Marsh is dead.

It keeps happening. Brennan snaps that everyone Ward names ends up in a body bag, so someone must be feeding the killer Ward's board. Ward moves the board home, where he talks through his cases out loud at night, and the deaths continue.

Then he finds a listening bug in his apartment, and it's police-issue. The killer has someone inside the department, and soon the rumor is in the papers.

## Act 2: The Mole

Ruth starts acting wrong. She asks where Ward was last night, photographs his board when she thinks he isn't looking, and gets spotted near a victim's building.

Ward pins his next suspect, Dennis Pryce, and Brennan puts a cop on Pryce's door. The next morning, for the first time, nobody's dead. The pattern broke.

Then Ruth arrests Ward. The bug was hers, and it caught his door opening around 3 a.m. on every murder night, and again last night. Anyone who suspected Ward feels brilliant.

Ward spends the night in a holding cell and sleeps soundly for the first time in months. Meanwhile, the arrest leaks: a cop has been taken in on the Night Judge case.

Pryce already believes the killer has help inside the department, so he stops trusting the cop at his door. At 2 a.m. he tries to sneak out down an icy fire escape. Neighbors hear screaming, and Pryce is found dead in the alley below his open window, his apartment torn apart.

Everyone, Ward included, is certain the Night Judge did it while Ward sat in a cell. Ward is released, Ruth is suspended for the illegal bug, and her tapes look like a frame job. Even the skeptics believe him now.

Pryce's landlord mentions a man in a long coat who flashed a badge to get into the basement a few nights earlier. To Ward, that proves what the papers are saying: the killer is a cop.

That night's nightmare is the only one with no fight. You stand in Pryce's basement, staring, and nothing happens.

Days later, Ward catches Ruth outside his building at 3 a.m., watching his window. Everything points at her: she had access to the board, she bugged him, she tried to frame him, and now she's stalking him. You pin Ruth.

## Act 3: The Last Pin

Brennan gives Ruth a protection detail, as he now does for anyone Ward pins. That night's nightmare breaks every rule so far: it's set in Ruth's apartment, a place you've never investigated.

Lily stands in front of the door. The monster fights harder than any before it, and there's no retry. Midway through, the door behind Lily bursts open from the outside, shadow figures pour through, and she dissolves.

Cut to the interrogation room. "We finally got him," Ward says. "At Ruth's place."

## The reveal

The voice asks one last question: "Who killed Victor Hale?"

Ward points at the silhouette. "He did."

"Then turn off your lamp and look at him."

You press the button. Ward's lamp goes out, the lights come up on the other side, and the silhouette vanishes. Through the glass are Ruth at the microphone with her arm in a sling, Captain Brennan, and Dr. Reyes.

The silhouette was Ward's own reflection, turned into a shadow by the lamp at his back. He was never behind the glass. He was in front of it, cuffed to the table.

The first line of the game was "Tell us about the killer." Now Ruth's voice comes through clear for the first time: "You just did." Then: "You described every murder, Elias. You just called them dreams."

Ruth comes in and sets two things on the table. The first is a framed photo that hung on the wall in one of the nightmares, where the real crime scene had only an empty nail. Police found it in a box in Ward's closet, with something from every scene, each bagged and labeled like evidence.

The second is the envelope. For the first time, the prompt says Open. Inside are new DNA results from Lily's case.

Victor Hale is excluded. The DNA matches Walter Doyle, arrested last month on an unrelated assault charge. The postmark is the day before Hale died.

Then comes the memory Ward buried for six years. It's late, he's drunk, and someone knocks on his door. He doesn't get up: "Not tonight, kid."

Lily's voice comes through the wood, scared: "Please, Mr. Ward. It's Walt. I'm scared of him." The screen cuts to black on the sound of her footsteps going back down the hall.

That's what she was whispering in every nightmare.

## The credits

The credits roll over the case board. One by one, every red string you tied snaps loose and re-ties to a single photo: Ward's mugshot.

Then your nightmares replay without the filter: your exact inputs, in real apartments, on real people. Lily is never there. Each time, it's just someone running for their own front door, and you getting in the way.

The last replay is the opening nightmare, in Hale's apartment at 3:40 a.m. You killed Victor Hale in the first five minutes of the game.

## What really happened, in order


1. Six years ago, Lily knocked on Ward's door and named Walt. Ward, drunk, said "Not tonight," and Walt killed her that night. Ward never told anyone about the knock; he fixed on Hale, interviewed Walt for five minutes, and cleared him.
2. Last month, Ruth had Lily's evidence retested with newer methods, hoping to prove Ward right. Walt's DNA had just entered the database after his assault arrest. The lab mailed the results to the detective of record: Ward.
3. Ward never opened the envelope, but part of him knew. That night came his first dissociative fugue: he killed Hale at 3:40 a.m. so the case could never be reopened. He left the window open for hours, then closed it and cranked the radiator, so the warm room fooled the coroner into guessing 11 p.m.
4. Ruth told Brennan when the envelope had arrived. Brennan refused to look at Ward, because if Ward was wrong about Hale, every case he ever closed would be reopened. So Ruth bugged Ward's apartment herself.
5. From then on, whoever Ward became certain about, his fugue-self visited that night. They opened the door because he was a cop; he killed them, took something, and bagged and labeled it at home. The night after he worked each scene as a detective, the memory surfaced as a nightmare, with Lily guarding the door.
6. Pryce had a cop at his door, so Ward's fugue-self flashed his badge to the landlord and cased the basement for another way in. Before he could come back, Ruth arrested him, the arrest leaked, and Pryce panicked and fell. It was the only death Ward didn't commit, and Ruth's arrest caused it.
7. Suspended, Ruth worked out the rule: whoever Ward is certain about dies. She made herself look guilty so he'd pin her, and had her protection detail wait in the apartment across the hall instead of at her door. When Ward knocked at 3 a.m., she opened the door, like every victim before her.
8. In custody, Ward's mind cast him as the detective. Dr. Reyes had Ruth let him tell it his way, about "the killer," in the third person. The confession was never the case; the box in his closet was, and the interrogation was to bring him back.

## Clues, all visible on the first playthrough


- The envelope's postmark is readable from the first scene.
- Hale's windowsill plants are frost-killed, in an apartment where the radiator is blasting.
- The clock in every nightmare shows the real time of death, which never matches the coroner's report.
- No crime scene has forced entry, because every victim opened the door for their killer.
- Lily always stands in front of the door, and the monster never touches her. It's trying to reach the door behind her.
- The monster's roar is someone begging, pitched down and distorted. Pitch it back up and you hear "please."
- The nightmares show things the investigation never found, like the framed photo where the crime scene had only an empty nail.
- Walt asks "Out late again?", and something in the apartment is always wrong in the morning.
- Ward only blacks out when he's alone and free to leave. In the holding cell, he sleeps fine.
- The man in the basement flashed a badge.
- The only nightmare with no fight follows the only death Ward didn't commit.
- Ruth's nightmare is the first one set somewhere you haven't investigated.
- There isn't a single mirror anywhere in the game. The first one you see is the glass in the opening scene.
- The silhouette only ever moves when Ward does.
- Ward's old case file on Lily has one line about Walt: "Building super. Interviewed. No further action."

## Rules the game runs on


- Whoever you pin dies that night. Keep it to two or three credible suspects per chapter, or the branching becomes too big to build.
- If the player refuses to pin anyone, Ward pins someone himself, off-screen: "I didn't need the board. I already knew." That person dies, and players who experiment get a huge clue: the killer isn't reading the board, he's reading Ward.
- Secret ending: Ward's police ID sits in your inventory all game, and the board accepts any photo. Pin Ward, and that night he doesn't come home; he walks into the precinct at 3 a.m., confesses, and opens the envelope himself. Everyone who would have died lives, and it's the only case in the game you can actually solve.

## Why it lands

The double bluff does the heavy lifting. Suspicious players are "proved" right by Ruth's arrest and "proved" wrong by Pryce's death, so by Act 3 even the skeptics have stopped looking at Ward. Smart players who guess "delayed method" are wrong too, because there was no method.

Every suspect you pinned was innocent, because Ward committed every murder they were accused of. In a detective game, you were wrong about every single person, and every wrong answer got someone killed. The game never lied to make you feel right; each death just felt like proof you were getting close.

Underneath it all, the theme is certainty. Ward, Brennan, and the player all see what they're sure of. Ruth is the only one who tests her certainty, and she bets her own life on it instead of someone else's.

The only time Ward was ever right, he was pointing at his own reflection. And the one time someone told him exactly who the killer was, he said "Not tonight."
